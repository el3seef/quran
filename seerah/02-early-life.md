## lineage
title: His Lineage
word: 'Adnan
mood: parchment
when: Before his birth
y: 496
place: Makkah
look: lineage
tags: family
Scholars speak of his lineage in three parts. The line back to 'Adnan is agreed on by all the historians and genealogists. The line from 'Adnan back to Ibrahim has names that differ between sources. The line from Ibrahim back to Adam is less sure, and parts of it are certainly mistaken.

The agreed line runs: Muhammad, son of 'Abdullah, son of 'Abdul-Muttalib (whose name was Shaibah), son of Hashim (named 'Amr), son of 'Abd Manaf (named Al-Mughirah), son of Qusai (named Zaid). Then Kilab, Murrah, Ka'b, Lu'ai and Ghalib. Then Fihr, called Quraish, after whom the tribe is named. Then Malik, An-Nadr (named Qais), Kinanah, Khuzaimah, Mudrikah (named 'Amir), Ilyas, Mudar, Nizar, Ma'ad and 'Adnan.
lineage: 'Adnan > Ma'ad > Nizar > Mudar > Ilyas > Mudrikah > Khuzaimah > Kinanah > An-Nadr > Malik > Fihr (Quraish) > Ghalib > Lu'ai > Ka'b > Murrah > Kilab > Qusai > 'Abd Manaf > Hashim > 'Abdul-Muttalib > 'Abdullah > Muhammad ﷺ

## hashim
title: Hashim and 'Abdul-Muttalib
word: Hashim
mood: parchment
when: Died 497 CE
y: 497
place: Makkah, Madinah and Gaza
look: map hashim
tags: family
His family is called Hashimite after his great-grandfather Hashim, who fed and watered the pilgrims. His name was 'Amr. He was called Hashim, "the one who breaks bread", because he crumbled bread into broth for the pilgrims. He was the first to lead Quraish's two great caravans, in winter and summer [106:1-2]. On a trade journey he married Salma of Banu 'Adi bin An-Najjar in Madinah. He went on to Syria and died at Gaza in 497 CE while she was carrying his child.

Their son was named Shaibah for the white hair on his head, and he grew up among his mother's people in Madinah. When his uncle Al-Muttalib heard of him, he went to fetch him, weeping when he first saw him. He brought him home on his camel. Makkah took the boy for Al-Muttalib's servant, and so he came to be called 'Abdul-Muttalib.

After Al-Muttalib died, another uncle, Nawfal, seized 'Abdul-Muttalib's rights. His mother's people rode in from Madinah, eighty horsemen strong, and made Nawfal give them back before the elders of Quraish. Khuza'ah then joined Banu Hashim in a pact against Nawfal and 'Abd Shams. Long after, that pact would become a cause of the conquest of Makkah.

## zamzam
title: Zamzam Found Again
word: Zamzam
mood: night
img: zamzam
when: Before 570 CE
y: 540
place: Makkah
look: image zamzam
tags: makkah, family
In a dream, 'Abdul-Muttalib was told to dig at a certain spot. There he found the well of Zamzam, which Jurhum had filled in when they left Makkah. With it he found the swords, armour and two golden deer they had buried. The swords and the deer were beaten into the door of the Ka'bah, and from then on Zamzam's water was given to the pilgrims.

Quraish claimed a share in the well. 'Abdul-Muttalib refused: Allah had given it to him alone. They set out to have it judged by a diviner of Banu Sa'd. On the way, signs from Allah confirmed his right to it.

During this quarrel, 'Abdul-Muttalib vowed that if he were given ten sons who grew to manhood, he would sacrifice one of them at the Ka'bah.

## elephant
title: The Year of the Elephant
word: Al-Fil
mood: dusk
when: Muharram, early 571 CE
y: 571.1
place: From San'a' to Makkah
look: map elephant
tags: makkah, signs
refs: 105:1-5
Abrahah, the Abyssinian ruler of Yemen, saw the Arabs making pilgrimage to the Ka'bah. To turn them away from it, he built a great church in San'a'. A man of Kinanah crept into it by night and defiled it. In fury, Abrahah marched on Makkah with sixty thousand men to destroy the Ka'bah. He rode the largest of his nine to thirteen elephants.

At the valley of Muhassir, between Muzdalifah and Mina, the elephant knelt and would not move. Turned north, south or east, it went quickly. Turned toward the Ka'bah, it knelt. Then Allah sent birds in flocks, each carrying three stones of baked clay, one in its beak and two in its claws. The stones tore Abrahah's men apart [105:1-5]. Abrahah himself fell sick, and his fingertips had to be cut away. He reached San'a' in a wretched state and died there. Quraish had fled to the hills, and came home safely.

This was in Muharram, fifty or fifty-five days before the Prophet's ﷺ birth. The news reached every corner of the known world, and showed that Allah had chosen this House.
facts:
- His army: about 60,000
- Elephants: 9 to 13
- The month: Muharram, about 50 days before his birth

## abdullah
title: 'Abdullah, the Ransomed
word: 100
mood: parchment
img: camels
when: Before 571 CE
y: 570
place: Makkah
look: text
tags: family
'Abdul-Muttalib had ten sons, among them Al-Harith, Az-Zubair, Abu Talib, 'Abdullah, Hamzah, Abu Lahab and Al-'Abbas, and six daughters. When the ten were grown, he told them of his vow, and they accepted it. Their names were drawn by arrows before the idol Hubal, and the arrow fell on 'Abdullah. He was his father's most loved son, the finest and the most chaste.

'Abdul-Muttalib took him to the Ka'bah with a blade, but Quraish, 'Abdullah's uncles of Makhzum and his brother Abu Talib pleaded with him. A diviner told them to draw between 'Abdullah and ten camels, adding ten more camels each time the arrow fell on him. At one hundred camels the arrow fell on the camels, and they were sacrificed in his place. After this, blood money among the Arabs rose from ten camels to a hundred, and Islam later kept it.

'Abdul-Muttalib married him to Aminah, daughter of Wahb, chief of Banu Zuhrah, a woman of the noblest descent. Soon after, 'Abdullah was sent to Madinah to buy dates (or, in another report, was coming home from Syria) and died there, aged twenty-five, most likely two months before his son was born. He left five camels, a few goats, and a servant, Barakah, known as Umm Ayman, who would nurse the Prophet ﷺ.

## birth
title: His Birth
word: 571
mood: white
img: kaaba-1880

y: 571.3
place: The quarter of Banu Hashim, Makkah
look: image makkah
tags: family, signs
He was born in the quarter of Banu Hashim in Makkah on a Monday morning, the ninth of Rabi' al-Awwal, in the Year of the Elephant. This was the fortieth year of the reign of the Persian king Kisra Anushirwan, the twentieth or twenty-second of April, 571 CE.

His mother said that at his birth a light came out of her that lit up the palaces of Syria. Signs are told of that night: balconies of Kisra's palace falling and the sacred fire of the Magians going out. But none of these reports has a confirmed chain.

She sent word to his grandfather, who came full of joy, carried him to the Ka'bah, prayed to Allah and thanked Him. He named him Muhammad, a name the Arabs seldom used. After his mother, the first to nurse him was Thuwaibah, a freed slave of Abu Lahab, who had nursed his uncle Hamzah too.
facts:
- Day: Monday morning
- Date: 9 Rabi' al-Awwal, Year of the Elephant
- In the calendar: 20 or 22 April, 571 CE
- Named by: his grandfather, 'Abdul-Muttalib

## halimah
title: Halimah of Banu Sa'd
word: Halimah
mood: oasis
img: acacia
when: 571 – 575 CE
y: 572
place: The desert of Banu Sa'd
look: image desert
tags: family
The townspeople sent their babies to wet nurses in the desert, to grow strong in open air and learn the pure speech of the Bedouin. His nurse was Halimah, daughter of Abu Dhu'aib, of Banu Sa'd bin Bakr. Her husband was Al-Harith bin 'Abdul-'Uzza.

It was a year of drought. Halimah came to Makkah on a lean donkey with an old camel that gave no milk, and a baby who cried all night from hunger. Every nurse turned the orphan away, expecting nothing from a boy with no father. Halimah would not go home empty-handed, so she took him. At once her milk was plenty for him and for her own son. The old camel gave milk, the family slept through the night, and her husband said she had taken a blessed child.

The lean donkey outran the whole caravan home. Their land turned green and their flocks came home full. After two years they weaned him, and they begged his mother to let them keep him longer. He stayed until he was about four or five. His foster sister Ash-Shaima' used to care for him.

## chest

Anas reports, in Sahih Muslim, that Jibreel came to the boy while he played with other children. He laid him down and opened his chest, took out his heart, and removed a clot from it, saying, "That was Satan's share of you." He washed the heart in Zamzam water in a golden basin and returned it to its place.

The children ran to his nurse crying that Muhammad had been killed. They found him unharmed, only pale. Anas said he had seen the mark of it on his chest.

Halimah was frightened after this, and took him back to his mother.

## aminah

He lived with his mother until he was six. To honour her husband's memory, Aminah set out to visit his grave in Yathrib, about five hundred kilometres away. She took the boy, 'Abdul-Muttalib and their servant Umm Ayman. They stayed a month.

On the way home she fell gravely ill and died at Al-Abwa', on the road between Makkah and Madinah. 'Abdul-Muttalib brought the boy back to Makkah, now without father or mother.

## grandfather
title: In His Grandfather's Shade
word: Shaibah
mood: sand
when: 577 – 579 CE
y: 578
place: Makkah
look: text
tags: family
His grandfather loved him more than his own children. A mattress was laid for 'Abdul-Muttalib in the shade of the Ka'bah. His sons sat around it out of respect, but the boy sat on it. When his uncles moved him, 'Abdul-Muttalib would say, "Leave my grandson. By Allah, he will have a great standing," and he would stroke his back.

'Abdul-Muttalib died in Makkah when the boy was eight years, two months and ten days old. His care passed to Abu Talib, his father's full brother. Abu Talib put him before his own children and protected him for forty years. He judged other men by how they treated his nephew.

Once, in a year without rain, Quraish asked Abu Talib to pray for it. He went to the Ka'bah with the boy, who had a cloud above him, and they stood by its wall and prayed. Clouds gathered from every side, and the rain came pouring down.

## bahirah
title: Bahirah the Monk
word: Busra
mood: dusk
when: About 583 CE
y: 583
place: Busra, on the road to Syria
look: map busra
tags: signs, journeys
At twelve he went with Abu Talib on a trade journey to Syria. At Busra, in the Hawran under Roman rule, a monk named Bahirah came out to meet the caravan, though he had never met them before. He took the boy's hand and said he was the master of all people, whom Allah would send as a mercy to the worlds.

Abu Talib asked how he knew. The monk said every stone and tree had bowed as they came over the pass, which they do only for a prophet. He also recognized the seal of prophethood below his shoulder, like an apple, as described in their books. He urged Abu Talib to take the boy home, for fear of the Romans and the Jews. Abu Talib sent him back to Makkah with some of his servants.

## fijar

When he was about twenty, war broke out between Quraish and Kinanah on one side and Qais 'Ailan on the other. It went on for years with heavy losses. It was called Al-Fijar, "the profane war", because it was fought in the sacred months, when fighting was forbidden. Harb bin Umaiyah led Quraish.

He was present in one of its battles to help his uncles, gathering the enemy's arrows as they fell and handing them back.

## fudul
title: The Pact of the Virtuous
word: Fudul
mood: parchment
when: Dhul-Qa'dah, after the Fijar
y: 591.5
place: The house of 'Abdullah bin Jud'an, Makkah
look: quote
tags: society
When peace returned, the clans of Hashim, Al-Muttalib, Asad, Zuhrah and Taim met in the house of 'Abdullah bin Jud'an. They pledged to put down injustice and to protect the rights of the weak and the poor. Tribal pride had never bound the Arabs to anything like it.

It began with a merchant of Zubaid, who sold goods to Al-'As bin Wa'il and was never paid. No ally of his in Quraish would help. He climbed a hill and cried out his complaint in verse. Az-Zubair bin 'Abdul-Muttalib took up his cause, the clans met, and they made Al-'As pay.

As a prophet, he remembered it gladly. He said he had seen that pact in Ibn Jud'an's house, and it was dearer to him than the finest herds. If he were called to the like of it in Islam, he would answer.

In his youth he had no fixed trade. It is reported that he herded sheep among Banu Sa'd, and in Makkah for wages.

Khadijah, daughter of Khuwailid, was a woman of honour and wealth who paid men a share of the profit to trade for her. ## khadijah

Khadijah saw more profit and blessing from that journey than ever before. Maisarah told her of his manners, honesty, thoughtfulness and sincerity. Many great men of Quraish had asked to marry her, and she had refused them all. Now she told her friend Nafisah, daughter of Munyah, of her wish. Nafisah spoke to him, he agreed, and his uncles went to her uncle.

They were married before Banu Hashim and the chiefs of Mudar. His gift to her was twenty camels. She was forty, and the best of the women of Quraish in lineage, wealth and wisdom. She was his first wife, and he married no one else while she lived.

## kabah
title: Rebuilding the Ka'bah
word: Ka'bah
mood: parchment
when: 605 CE
y: 605
place: The Ka'bah, Makkah
look: kabah
tags: makkah
When he was thirty-five, Quraish rebuilt the Ka'bah. Since Isma'il's time it had been a low, roofless enclosure of stones, nine cubits high, and thieves could reach its treasure. Age had cracked its walls, and a great flood had almost brought it down.

They agreed to spend only clean money on it, nothing from usury, wrongdoing or ill-gotten gain. They were afraid to strike the first blow until Al-Walid bin Al-Mughirah began. When no harm came to him, the rest joined in, down to the foundations of Ibrahim. Each clan built its own part, and a Roman mason named Baqum laid the stones.

Then came the Black Stone. Every chief wanted the honour of setting it in place. For four or five days they quarrelled, and daggers were nearly drawn. The oldest of them, Abu Umaiyah bin Al-Mughirah, proposed that the first man to come through the gate should judge between them. It was Muhammad ﷺ. "Al-Amin, the trustworthy, has come," they said, "we accept him."

He spread a cloak, put the stone in it and had every chief lift its edges together. When it reached its place, he set it in with his own hands.

Their clean money ran short, so they left out about six cubits on the north side, which is the Hijr, or Hatim. They raised the door so they could decide who went in, and roofed the building on six columns.
facts:
- Height: about 15 metres
- Black Stone wall and the one facing it: 10 metres
- The other two walls: 12 metres
- The Black Stone: 1.5 metres above the ground
- The door: 2 metres above the ground

## al-amin
title: Al-Amin
word: Al-Amin
mood: white
when: Before 610 CE
y: 609
place: Makkah
look: quote
tags: character
In his youth he gathered the best qualities a man could have: sound judgement, deep thought, long silences and quiet reflection. He stayed away from wine, from meat sacrificed on the stone altars, and from the idols' festivals. He hated the idols, and could not bear to hear anyone swear by Al-Lat and Al-'Uzza.

Allah guarded him. Jabir reports, in Al-Bukhari, that while the Ka'bah was being rebuilt he carried stones with Al-'Abbas. Al-'Abbas told him to wrap his waist-cloth around his neck to pad his shoulders. As he did, he fell to the ground with his eyes turned to the sky. When he rose, he asked for his waist-cloth and tied it on again. He was never seen uncovered after that.

He was the most modest of his people and the most upright in his dealings. He was the gentlest, the most truthful, and the most faithful to his word, so that his people called him Al-Amin, the trustworthy. Khadijah said of him that he kept ties of kinship, carried the burdens of the weak, gave to the needy, honoured guests and stood by those who suffered for the truth.
