The caravan he had missed at Dhul-'Ushairah was on its way home from Syria. He sent Talhah bin 'Ubaidullah and Sa'id bin Zaid north to watch for it; at Al-Hawra' they saw Abu Sufyan pass, and hurried back. It carried fifty thousand gold dinars and was guarded by about forty men: a blow at it would shake Makkah's whole standing.

He urged the Muslims to go out and win back something of what they had been made to leave in Makkah, but he bound no one: whoever wished could stay. It seemed a small affair. ## damdam
title: "Your Merchandise!"
word: Help!
mood: ember

y: 624.185

Abu Sufyan travelled on edge, asking after Muhammad's movements at every stop. When his scouts told him the Muslims were waiting for him, he hired Damdam bin 'Amr Al-Ghifari to ride to Makkah for help. Damdam came into Makkah like a madman. He stood before the Ka'bah, cut off his camel's nose and ears, turned its saddle upside down, tore his shirt front and back, and screamed.

Makkah rose at once, its pride still stung by Nakhlah. Every man of standing went or sent someone in his place; only Abu Lahab stayed, sending a man who owed him money. Every clan of Quraish joined but Banu 'Adi. Soon thirteen hundred men were marching, a hundred of them on horses and six hundred in mail, slaughtering nine or ten camels a day to feed themselves.

They feared that Banu Bakr, their old enemies, would fall on Makkah behind them. Then Iblis came to them in the form of Suraqah bin Malik, a chief of Kinanah, and promised that no harm would reach them from behind.
quote: O Quraish! Your merchandise! It is with Abu Sufyan, and Muhammad and his companions are after it. Help! Help! — Damdam bin 'Amr Al-Ghifari, before the Ka'bah

## escape

They marched north boastfully, to be seen of men [8:47], past 'Usfan and Qudaid to Al-Juhfah. There another message reached them from Abu Sufyan: the caravan was safe, go home. He had turned off the main road towards the sea and slipped past.

Many wanted to turn back. Abu Jahl would not have it. They would go on to Badr, he said, and stay three nights there feasting, so that the Muslims would learn their lesson and the Arabs would know Quraish still ruled the land. Al-Akhnas bin Shuraiq took Banu Zahrah home anyway, and ever after they obeyed him in everything. Banu Hashim wanted to leave too, but Abu Jahl's threats kept them. A thousand men went on, and camped behind a dune at the far side of the valley of Badr.

The Prophet ﷺ had marched out on the main road towards Makkah, then turned left for Badr. At As-Safra' he sent two men of Juhainah ahead for news of the caravan. What came back was news of an army.

## council
title: "Advise Me"

Now the choice was hard. If they turned away, Quraish could march on to Madinah itself. Some of the believers were afraid, as if they were being driven to their death with their eyes open [8:5-6]. He told them how things stood and asked their counsel. Abu Bakr spoke well, and 'Umar after him.

Then Al-Miqdad bin 'Amr stood: they would not say, as the Children of Israel said to Musa, "Go, you and your Lord, and fight; we will sit here" [5:24]. They would say, "Go, you and your Lord, and fight, and we will fight with you." If he led them as far as Birk Al-Ghimad, they would fight beside him until he reached it.

He thanked him and prayed for him, and asked again, "Advise me, people." The three who had spoken were Emigrants; he meant the Helpers, the greater part of the army, whose pledge at Al-'Aqabah bound them only to defend him in their own city. Sa'd bin Mu'adh understood. "It seems you mean us." "Yes." Then Sa'd answered for them all.

"Go on, and be glad," he told them. "Allah has promised me one of the two parties. By Allah, it is as if I can see the places where they will fall."
quote: If you took us into this sea and plunged into it, we would plunge in with you; not one man of us would stay behind. Lead us on, with the blessing of Allah. — Sa'd bin Mu'adh, for the Helpers

## water-carriers

He rode out with Abu Bakr to scout, and from an old Bedouin they had out of him, without giving themselves away, where Quraish were camped. That evening he sent 'Ali, Az-Zubair and Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas to the wells of Badr. They caught two boys drawing water for Quraish and brought them back.

The boys said they were Quraish's water carriers. Some of the men wanted them to belong to the caravan, and beat them; so they said they were Abu Sufyan's, and were let alone. The Prophet ﷺ, who had been praying, finished and was angry. He spoke with them a little, and learned from them how many Quraish were, where they were camped, and which of their chiefs had come. "Here is Makkah," he told his men, "sending you the dearest of its lives."

## rain
title: Rain in the Night

That night it rained on both camps. For Quraish it was a hindrance that held them back. For the Muslims it was a blessing that washed them, firmed the ground beneath their feet and steadied their hearts [8:11].

They moved up and stopped at the nearest well of Badr. Al-Hubab bin Al-Mundhir asked whether Allah had put him in this place, so that they could not move from it, or whether it was a matter of war and planning. War and planning, he said. Then this was no place to stop, said Al-Hubab: let them camp at the well nearest the enemy, build a cistern there, and stop up the wells behind it, so that they would have water and Quraish would have none. He agreed, and it was done in the night.

Sa'd bin Mu'adh asked that a shelter be built for him, to command the army from: if Allah gave them victory, all was well; if not, he could go back to the many in Madinah who loved him and would have come had they known. Sa'd stood guard over it with a band of the Helpers. The Prophet ﷺ walked over the ground, pointing: here so-and-so will be tomorrow, if Allah wills, and here so-and-so. Then he spent the night in prayer beside a tree, while the army slept the deep sleep Allah sent over them as a security from Him.

- The rain: a hindrance to Quraish, a mercy to the Muslims
- The wells: taken on Al-Hubab bin Al-Mundhir's advice
- The shelter: proposed by Sa'd bin Mu'adh, guarded by the Helpers
- The sleep: sent by Allah over the Muslims, as a security from Him

## ranks
title: Face to Face

mood: iron

Quraish took their place on the far side of the valley. A few of them came down to draw water at the Muslims' wells; all were shot down but Hakim bin Hizam, who would one day be a devoted Muslim. 'Umair bin Wahb rode round the Muslim camp and came back: about three hundred men, no reinforcements behind them and no ambush, men who would not surrender and would take many with them before they fell. That, and the ties of kin between the two sides, took the heart out of some. 'Utbah bin Rabi'ah spoke for turning back. Abu Jahl answered with a cry for revenge for the man killed at Nakhlah, and carried the rest with him.

The Prophet ﷺ told them not to begin until he gave the word, to spend their arrows sparingly, and not to draw swords until the enemy was close. Abu Jahl prayed that whichever side cut the ties of kin and brought what was not known be destroyed that day. The answer had already come [8:19].

The first of Quraish to come forward was Al-Aswad bin 'Abdul-Asad Al-Makhzumi, a hot-tempered man who swore he would drink from the Muslims' cistern or destroy it or die for it. Hamzah met him, cut through his leg, and finished him at the cistern's edge.
quote: O Allah, here are Quraish, come in their pride and arrogance, defying You and belying Your Messenger. O Allah, Your help, which You promised me. — The Prophet ﷺ, as the armies came in sight

'Utbah bin Rabi'ah stepped out between his brother Shaibah and his son Al-Walid and called for single combat. Three young men of the Helpers went out: 'Awf and Mu'awwidh, the sons of Al-Harith, and 'Abdullah bin Rawahah. Quraish wanted none of them: they wanted their own cousins. So the Prophet ﷺ called on 'Ubaidah bin Al-Harith, on his uncle Hamzah and on his cousin 'Ali.

It was quickly over. Hamzah killed Shaibah, and 'Ali killed Al-Walid. 'Ubaidah was badly wounded, and before he fell Hamzah turned on 'Utbah and killed him. 'Ali and Hamzah carried 'Ubaidah back, his leg cut off. He died four or five days later. 'Ali used to swear that the ayah about the two opposing parties who disputed over their Lord [22:19] was revealed about them.

The duels had cost Quraish some of their best, and in fury they came on all together. The Muslims held their ground as he had told them, and the charge broke on them.

In the shelter he turned to the qiblah, raised his hands and called on his Lord, again and again, until his cloak slipped from his shoulders. Abu Bakr picked it up and put it back on his shoulders. "Enough, Prophet of Allah. You have pleaded with your Lord enough. He will keep what He promised you."

## angels

He dozed a moment, then raised his head. "Good news, Abu Bakr. Allah's help has come. There is Jibreel on his horse, in the thick of the dust." He came out of the shelter reciting that the host would be routed and turn their backs [54:45], took a handful of pebbles at Jibreel's word, threw it towards Quraish and said, "Confusion seize their faces!" A storm of dust blew into their eyes. You did not throw when you threw, but Allah threw [8:17].

Only now did he give the order to charge, telling them of a Paradise as wide as the heavens and the earth [3:133]. A boy of sixteen named 'Umair was eating some dates. "These are holding me back from Paradise," he cried, threw them away, ran into the fighting and was killed.

The Companions told of what they saw. A man chasing an idolater heard a whip crack above him and a voice cry, "Forward, Haizum!", and saw the man fall dead before him; "That was help from the third heaven," the Prophet ﷺ told him. Al-'Abbas, brought in a prisoner by a man of the Helpers, said it was not this man who had taken him, but a bald rider with the most handsome face, on a piebald horse, whom he could not see among them. "Be quiet," the Prophet ﷺ told the Helper. "Allah strengthened you with a noble angel."
quote: Good news, Abu Bakr: Allah's help has come. There is Jibreel on his horse, in the thick of the dust. — The Prophet ﷺ

## abu-jahl
title: The Pharaoh of This Nation
word: Pharaoh
mood: ember
when: 17 Ramadan 2 AH

tags: battles, makkah
Iblis, in Suraqah's form, saw the angels and fled, saying he saw what they did not see [8:48]; though Quraish begged him to stay, he ran and threw himself into the sea. Quraish's ranks began to break. Retreat became flight; they threw away their armour and left their camp and their camels, while the Muslims pursued, killing and taking captives.

Abu Jahl stood his ground, begging them by Al-Lat and Al-'Uzza to hold, until only a knot of men stood round him. 'Abdur-Rahman bin 'Awf was in the ranks between two boys of the Helpers. One whispered to him to show him Abu Jahl: he wanted to fight him until one of them was dead. The other whispered the same. 'Abdur-Rahman could hardly believe it. He pointed him out, and they ran at him together and struck him down. Each came back claiming to have killed him. The Prophet ﷺ looked at their swords. "You both killed him."

When it was over, 'Abdullah bin Mas'ud found him with his last breath in him, and put his foot on his neck: had Allah disgraced him now? "Disgraced? I am only a man killed by his own people. Tell me, who won?" "Allah and His Messenger." "You have climbed a hard road, little shepherd," said Abu Jahl, for Ibn Mas'ud had once herded the flocks of Makkah's nobles. Ibn Mas'ud brought his head to the Prophet ﷺ, who praised Allah, Who had kept His promise, helped His servant and routed the parties alone.
quote: This is the Pharaoh of this nation. — The Prophet ﷺ, over Abu Jahl's body
people:

Badr set kin against kin, and the Companions' hearts were laid bare. Before the battle he had told them to spare some men of Banu Hashim and others who had come out unwillingly, among them his uncle Al-'Abbas and Abul-Bukhtari bin Hisham, who had never harmed him in Makkah and had worked to end the boycott.

Abu Hudhaifah bin 'Utbah cried out: they were to kill their fathers, sons and brothers, and spare Al-'Abbas? If he met him he would put his sword in him. The Prophet ﷺ turned to 'Umar: "Should the face of the Messenger's uncle be struck with a sword?" Abu Hudhaifah never stopped fearing what he had said, until he was killed a martyr at Al-Yamamah.

When the dead of Quraish were dragged to the well, the Prophet ﷺ saw Abu Hudhaifah's face fall as his father 'Utbah was carried past. He had never doubted his father deserved his end, Abu Hudhaifah said; he had only hoped he would be guided. The Prophet ﷺ prayed for him and spoke kindly to him.

- Sa'd bin Mu'adh: when captives were taken, he said he would sooner have seen the idolaters killed in this first battle than spared

## well

Fourteen Muslims were killed at Badr, six Emigrants and eight Helpers. Of Quraish seventy were killed and seventy taken captive, and among the dead lay many of their leaders and the bitterest of his enemies, Abu Jahl first among them.

He had the bodies of twenty-four of the leaders of Quraish thrown into an old well. On the third day he went and stood at its edge and called them by their names and their fathers' names. You were an evil kin to your Prophet, he said: you called me a liar when others believed me, you abandoned me when others helped me, you drove me out when others took me in. Would it not have been better had you obeyed Allah and His Messenger? We have found what our Lord promised us to be true; have you found what your Lord promised you to be true?

"Messenger of Allah," said 'Umar, "why speak to bodies without souls?"
quote: By Him in Whose hand is Muhammad's soul, you do not hear what I say any better than they do. — The Prophet ﷺ, at the well of Badr

## news

Quraish fled home in scattered groups, too ashamed to face their people. The first to reach Makkah was Al-Haisuman bin 'Abdullah Al-Khuza'i, naming the dead chief by chief; at first they thought he had gone mad.

Abu Lahab heard the whole story from Abu Sufyan bin Al-Harith, who told him of the angels' part in it. Abu Lahab flew into a rage and fell to beating Abu Rafi', a Muslim, who was there. Umm Al-Fadl, a Muslim woman, took a stick and split his head open. Seven days later he died of a foul ulcer; his sons left him three days unburied for fear of it, then took him to a pit and threw stones and earth over him from a distance. In Makkah there was hardly a house without its dead or its captive, and Quraish forbade weeping aloud, so that the Muslims would not rejoice to hear it.

To Madinah he sent 'Abdullah bin Rawahah and Zaid bin Harithah with the good news. The Jews and hypocrites had spread word that he was dead, for Zaid was riding his camel Al-Qaswa'. The news reached the city as they finished burying Ruqaiyah, his daughter. 'Uthman, her husband, had stayed behind at the Prophet's word to nurse her.

- In Madinah: rumours of his death, then 'Abdullah bin Rawahah and Zaid with the truth
- Ruqaiyah: buried the day the news came; 'Uthman had stayed to nurse her

## spoils

The spoils caused the first quarrel. Those who had gathered them, those who had chased the enemy and those who had guarded the Prophet ﷺ each claimed them, for no rule had yet been given. He held them back until the ayah came: the spoils belong to Allah and the Messenger, so fear Allah and set things right between you [8:1]. On the way home he divided them equally, after the fifth. At As-Safra' he had An-Nadr bin Al-Harith put to death, and at 'Irq Az-Zabyah 'Uqbah bin Abi Mu'ait, the man who had laid an animal's entrails on him as he prayed. "And who will look after my children?" 'Uqbah asked. "The Fire."

At Ar-Rawha' the people of Madinah came out to meet him, and many there became Muslims after it. He told them to treat the captives well, and they gave them their bread and ate the dates themselves. He asked Abu Bakr and 'Umar about them. Abu Bakr said to take ransom: they were kin, the money would strengthen the Muslims, and Allah might guide them. 'Umar said to kill their leaders. He took Abu Bakr's advice. The next day 'Umar found the two of them weeping: an ayah had come rebuking them for taking ransom before the enemy was crushed in the land [8:67-68]; but Allah had already allowed ransom [47:4], so no punishment came.

- Suhail bin 'Amr: 'Umar wanted his front teeth pulled so he could not speak against Islam; the Prophet ﷺ refused
- Sa'd bin An-Nu'man: held in Makkah, exchanged for Abu Sufyan's son 'Amr

Surat Al-Anfal came down about Badr, a commentary on the battle: on the help Allah sent, on the faults still left in the believers' hearts, on what Allah wanted the believers to be in war and in peace, unlike the Arabs of the Ignorance.

The fast of Ramadan had been made obligatory that same year, and zakat to ease the poverty of the Emigrants. Days after the victory came the first 'Eid al-Fitr. The Muslims went out of their houses praising Allah aloud, glorifying Him for His favour and the help He had sent them.
