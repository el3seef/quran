## kab-zuhair
title: Ka'b bin Zuhair

Ka'b bin Zuhair, of a family of poets and among the finest poets of the Arabs, had mocked the Prophet ﷺ in verse. After Ta'if his brother Bujair wrote to him: the Prophet ﷺ had put to death some in Makkah who had mocked and harmed him, and the poets who were left had scattered for their lives; if he valued his skin, let him come to him, for he never killed a man who came repenting. They wrote back and forth until the earth felt narrow to Ka'b.

He came to Madinah and stayed with a man of Juhainah, prayed Fajr with him, and went and sat by the Prophet ﷺ, who did not know his face, and put his hand in his. "Messenger of Allah, if Ka'b bin Zuhair came to you repenting, as a Muslim, would you grant him safety if I brought him?" "Yes." "I am Ka'b bin Zuhair." A Helper jumped up to ask leave to cut off his head. "Leave him: he has come repenting, and put the past away."

Then Ka'b recited the poem that begins with Su'ad, praising the Prophet ﷺ and asking his pardon. He praised the Emigrants in it, and spoke ill of the Helpers for the man who had wanted his head; later he made it up to them in another poem.
quote: Leave him alone; he has come repenting, and has put away the past. — The Prophet ﷺ, of Ka'b bin Zuhair

'Uyainah bin Hisn rode against Banu Tamim with fifty horsemen, none of them Emigrants or Helpers, marching by night and hiding by day, and fell on them in the desert. They fled, and he brought back eleven men, twenty-one women and thirty boys, who were lodged in the house of Ramlah bint Al-Harith.

Ten of their chiefs came to Madinah and shouted at the Prophet's door: "Muhammad, come out to us!" He came out and talked with them, then left them to pray Dhuhr, and afterwards sat in the courtyard of the mosque. They had come to boast. Their orator 'Utarid bin Hajib spoke, and the Prophet ﷺ had Thabit bin Qais bin Shammas answer him. Their poet Az-Zibriqan bin Badr recited, and Hassan bin Thabit answered him on the spot.

When it was over, Al-Aqra' bin Habis said it all: their orator was better than ours, their poet more gifted, their voices finer. They accepted Islam, and he treated them generously and gave back their women and children.

## adi
title: 'Adi bin Hatim
word: 'Adi
mood: parchment

y: 630.47
place: The land of Tai', and Madinah
look: quote

He sent 'Ali with a hundred and fifty men, a black flag and a white banner, to pull down Al-Fuls, the idol of Tai'. At dawn they fell on the clan of Hatim, broke the idol and took captives and herds. 'Adi bin Hatim had fled; his sister was taken. In Madinah she begged the Prophet ﷺ to be kind to her: her brother was gone and her father dead, and she was old and could do no work. Who was her brother? 'Adi bin Hatim. "The man who ran from Allah and His Messenger?" and he walked on. The next day she asked again, and the third day he granted it, and gave her a mount, and she went to Syria to find 'Adi. Go to him, she told her brother, willing or afraid: he had done a kindness her father would never have done.

'Adi came without protection or letter, and the Prophet ﷺ took him into his house. "What made you run? Did you run from saying there is no god but Allah? Do you know any god but Allah?" No. "Were you not a Rakusi, and did you not take a quarter of your people's spoils?" Yes. "That is not lawful in your own religion." Then 'Adi knew he was a prophet, who knew what was hidden, and he became a Muslim.

Once a man came complaining of poverty, and another of robbers on the roads. "'Adi, if you live long, you will see a woman ride alone from Al-Hirah to circle the Ka'bah, fearing no one but Allah; you will see the treasures of Kisra opened; and you will see a man hold out a handful of gold or silver, and find no one to take it." In his old age 'Adi said he had seen the woman ride from Al-Hirah, and he had been among those who opened Kisra's treasures.
quote: You will see a woman travel alone from Al-Hirah to circle the Ka'bah, fearing no one but Allah. — The Prophet ﷺ, to 'Adi bin Hatim

## rome
title: Rome Gathers
word: Rome
mood: iron
when: 9 AH · 630 CE
y: 630.55
place: Madinah, and the Syrian frontier
look: quote
tags: battles, kingdoms
Mu'tah had shown the Arab tribes of the north that they need not live under Caesar, and Caesar saw the danger on his borders. He resolved to crush the Muslims before they grew stronger, and gathered the Romans and the Ghassanid Arabs.

Madinah was uneasy. That year, when the Prophet ﷺ had sworn to keep away from his wives for a month, 'Umar's Helper neighbour came hammering at his door at night. "Have the Ghassanids come?" asked 'Umar. "Worse: the Messenger of Allah has divorced his wives." The hypocrites waited for the Muslims to fall. They built a mosque of their own, Masjid Ad-Dirar, the Mosque of Harm, and asked him to come and pray in it; he was setting out, and said on his return. Revelation told him what it really was, and when he came back he had it pulled down.

Nabataean traders bringing oil from Syria said Heraclius had gathered more than forty thousand men, with Lakhm, Judham and other tribes, and that his vanguard had reached Al-Balqa'. It was a year of drought and searing heat, and the road was long. But if he waited, Rome might come into the land of Islam, or to Madinah itself, the dying ways of the Ignorance would stir again, and the hypocrites would strike from behind. So, against his habit of hiding where he was going, he told the Muslims plainly: they were going to fight Rome.
quote: Have the Ghassanids come? — 'Umar, woken at night; so much did Madinah fear Rome that year

## hijr

On the way they passed Al-Hijr, where Thamud had carved their houses from the rock. The men drew water from its wells; he forbade them to drink it or make wudu' with it, told them to feed the dough they had kneaded with it to their camels, and to draw instead from the well where the she-camel of Salih used to drink. Do not enter the dwellings of those who wronged themselves, he said, unless you enter weeping, lest what befell them befall you; and he hurried his camel through the valley.

When they ran short of water he prayed, and a cloud came and rained until all had drunk. Near Tabuk he told them they would reach its spring tomorrow, not before the day was up, and no one was to touch its water until he came. Two men got there first; the spring was a thin trickle. He took a little of it in his hands, washed his face and hands, and poured it back, and the water gushed out for them all. "Mu'adh, if you live long, you will see this place filled with gardens." A wind would blow that night, he warned; let no one stand, and let every man tie his camel. One man stood, and the wind carried him off to the mountains of Tai'. All the way he joined Dhuhr with 'Asr and Maghrib with 'Isha'.

## tabuk-camp
title: Twenty Days at Tabuk
word: Tabuk
mood: sand
when: Rajab – Sha'ban 9 AH
y: 630.8
place: Tabuk

tags: battles, treaties, kingdoms
At Tabuk he spoke to the army, of the good of this world and the next, warning and giving hope, and lifted hearts worn down by hunger and thirst. But no army came. Hearing that the Muslims were marching, the Romans and their allies lost their nerve and melted back into their own lands. It gained the Muslims more than any battle could have.

Yuhannah bin Ru'bah, lord of Ailah, came to make peace and pay the jizyah, and so did the people of Jarba' and Adhruh. He gave each a writ of protection: in Allah's name, the protection of Allah and of Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah, for the people of Ailah, their ships and caravans by land and sea; none to be kept from any spring they were used to, nor any road they wished to travel.

He sent Khalid bin Al-Walid with four hundred and fifty horsemen to Ukaidir, lord of Dumat al-Jandal: "You will find him hunting wild cattle." It was a moonlit night, and as Khalid came in sight of his castle, wild cattle came and rubbed their horns against its gate, and Ukaidir came out to hunt them. Khalid took him from among his men and brought him to the Prophet ﷺ, who spared him and made peace on tribute. The tribes that had leaned on Rome turned towards the Muslims; the land of Islam now touched Rome's border.
facts:
- Ukaidir's tribute: two thousand camels, eight hundred head of cattle, four hundred coats of mail, four hundred spears
- Days at Tabuk: twenty
- The whole march: fifty days, Rajab to Ramadan
quote: You will find him hunting wild cattle. — The Prophet ﷺ, sending Khalid to Ukaidir

## three

Tabuk was a sifting. Whenever he was told someone had dropped behind on the march, he said, "Let him be: if there is good in him, Allah will bring him to you; if not, Allah has spared you of him."

Home again, he prayed two rak'ahs in the mosque and sat to receive people. More than eighty hypocrites came with excuses and oaths. He took them at their word, asked forgiveness for them, and left their hearts to Allah. Three believers had stayed behind with no excuse: Ka'b bin Malik, Murarah bin Ar-Rabi' and Hilal bin Umaiyah. They would not lie, and told him the truth. He told the Muslims not to speak to them until Allah judged them.

No one spoke to them. The earth for all its width grew narrow for them, and their own souls grew narrow. After forty days they were told to keep away from their wives. On the fiftieth day forgiveness came down [9:118]: the happiest day of their lives. And of those kept back by true need, weakness or illness, there was no blame [9:91]; as they neared Madinah he had said that some men there had been with them in every valley they crossed and every road they walked, held back only by their excuse.
quote: There are men in Madinah who were with you in every valley you crossed and on every road you travelled; only their excuse held them back. — The Prophet ﷺ, returning from Tabuk

After Tabuk everyone knew that only Islam would last in Arabia. ## thaqif
title: Thaqif and Al-Lat
word: Al-Lat
mood: sand
when: Ramadan 9 AH
y: 630.94
place: Madinah, and Ta'if
look: quote
tags: people, faith, companions
After Ta'if, 'Urwah bin Mas'ud Ath-Thaqafi had come to Madinah and become a Muslim. He was sure his people would follow him, for they loved him more than their own firstborn. When he called them to Islam they shot him from every side and killed him. Months later, unable to stand against the Muslim tribes around them, they sent 'Abd Yalil bin 'Amr, who would go only with five others, among them 'Uthman bin Abil-'As, the youngest.

A tent was pitched for them in a corner of the mosque, so that they could hear the Qur'an and watch the Muslims pray. They asked for a treaty that would let them keep fornication, wine and usury, keep Al-Lat, and be spared the prayer. He refused every one. They asked only not to have to break Al-Lat with their own hands, and that he granted. Each noon, while the others slept, young 'Uthman bin Abil-'As went to the Prophet ﷺ to learn the Qur'an, or to Abu Bakr if he was sleeping, and he made 'Uthman their chief. Years later, when Thaqif were about to apostatise, 'Uthman told them: you were the last to accept Islam, do not be the first to leave it.

At home the envoys pretended at first that war was coming, until their people's pride broke and they were ready to accept; then they told the truth. Khalid went with men to destroy Al-Lat. Al-Mughirah bin Shu'bah swore he would make them laugh at Thaqif: he struck it with his axe and fell down as if struck dead, and Thaqif cried that the goddess had killed him. He leapt up: Al-Lat was nothing but stones and dirt. They broke it to the ground, dug out its foundations, and carried off its jewels and robes.
quote: You were the last to accept Islam: do not be the first to leave it. — 'Uthman bin Abil-'As, to Thaqif

## najran
title: The Christians of Najran
word: Najran
mood: parchment
when: 9 AH
y: 631.22
place: Madinah
look: quote
tags: people, revelation, family
Najran, a land of seventy-three villages towards Yemen, could field a hundred thousand fighting men. Sixty of them came to Madinah, twenty-four of their nobility, and three who ruled them: the 'Aqib, 'Abdul-Masih, who governed; the Sayyid, Al-Aiham or Shurahbil, who saw to their affairs; and the bishop, Abu Harithah bin 'Alqamah. They talked with him, and when he called them to Islam and recited the Qur'an to them, they refused.

What did he say of 'Isa? He waited a day for the answer, and it came: the likeness of 'Isa with Allah is as the likeness of Adam, whom He created from dust; and whoever argues, let both sides bring their sons and women and selves, and pray that Allah's curse fall on whoever lies [3:59-61]. He gave them a day to think. When they still refused, he came out wrapping Al-Hasan and Al-Husain in his cloak, with Fatimah walking behind him, ready for the mutual curse.

The 'Aqib and the Sayyid took counsel: if he was a prophet and they cursed each other, neither they nor their children would ever prosper. They would not do it, and accepted his terms: two thousand garments a year, a thousand in Rajab and a thousand in Safar, with an ounce with each, and in return the covenant of Allah and His Messenger and freedom in their religion. They asked for a trustworthy man to collect it, and he sent Abu 'Ubaidah bin Al-Jarrah, the trustworthy one of this nation. In time Islam spread in Najran.
quote: The likeness of 'Isa with Allah is as the likeness of Adam: He created him from dust, then said to him, "Be," and he was. — Al 'Imran 3:59

With the delegation of Banu Hanifah came Musailimah, proud and wanting rule: if Muhammad made him his successor, he would follow him. The Prophet ﷺ went to him with a palm stalk in his hand and Thabit bin Qais beside him. "If you asked me for this stalk, I would not give it to you." Musailimah was killed at Al-Yamamah under Abu Bakr, by Wahshi, who had killed Hamzah. The other liar, Al-Aswad Al-'Ansi in Yemen, was killed by Fairuz a day and a night before the Prophet ﷺ died, and he told his Companions of it.

Banu 'Amir bin Sa'sa'ah sent 'Amir bin At-Tufail, the betrayer of Ma'unah, and Arbad bin Qais, who plotted to kill him: 'Amir kept him talking while Arbad drew his sword. Allah froze Arbad's hand on the hilt. On the way home a thunderbolt struck Arbad and his camel, and 'Amir died of a swelling like a camel's, in the house of a woman of Banu Salul.

## his-wars
title: A Look at His Battles

mood: white

None of his battles was lost through a fault of his plan, his ground or his choice of men. At Uhud the loss came from an order disobeyed, at Hunain from weakness in some of the ranks; and in both he stood his ground and turned the day. The commanders he trained went on to beat Persia and Rome. His wars brought security, made homes and work for the Emigrants, and armed the army, without a grain of injustice. They were not raids for plunder and captives, but a struggle to free the weak from oppression. And he bound them by rules.
list:
- Fight in the name of Allah, for His sake, those who disbelieve in Him; do not steal from the spoils, do not betray, do not mutilate, and do not kill a child

- If he came upon an enemy by night, he did not attack until morning
- No burning with fire; no killing of women or children
- Plunder is as unlawful as carrion
- Do not finish off the wounded, or chase the fleeing, or kill the captive; an envoy is never killed
- Whoever kills one under a covenant will not smell the fragrance of Paradise

'Amr bin Salamah remembered how his people lived by a water on the road, asking every traveller what this man was saying, until he knew the Qur'an by heart from their answers. The Arabs held back from Islam until the conquest: leave him to his own people, they said; if he beats them, he is a true prophet. When Makkah fell they hurried in, his own father first. He came back from the Prophet ﷺ with what to tell them: pray such a prayer at such a time; when the time comes, let one of you give the call, and let the one who knows most of the Qur'an lead you.

Ten thousand marched on Makkah; thirty thousand to Tabuk, less than a year later; and a hundred thousand or more stood with him at the Farewell Hajj, their "Here I am, O Allah" rolling to the horizon.

For more than twenty years he had stood up as he was told, to warn, and carried every burden of it: the struggle in men's hearts against the Ignorance, and the battles against those who would kill the faith before it took root. He fought it all by day and stood before his Lord by night.

Now the call had prevailed over all Arabia. The idols were not only abandoned but broken by the hands that once worshipped them. "There is no god but Allah" rang out five times a day across the desert, and reciters of the Qur'an went north and south. Scattered tribes became one people, the worshippers of created things worshippers of Allah alone, all of them His servants, brothers under His law.
quote: No Arab is better than a non-Arab, nor a non-Arab than an Arab, nor a red man than a black, except in piety. All are the children of Adam, and Adam was made of dust. — The Prophet ﷺ
