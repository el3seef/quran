## taif
title: The Road to Ta'if
word: Ta'if
mood: dusk
when: Shawwal, 10th year of prophethood · 619 CE
y: 619.85
place: Ta'if, about 60 km from Makkah
look: map taif
tags: journeys
At the end of May or the start of June 619, he set out on foot for Ta'if, about sixty kilometres from Makkah, with his freed slave Zaid bin Harithah, to call its people to Allah.

He went first to three brothers of the family of 'Umair, chiefs of Thaqif: 'Abd Yalil, Mas'ud and Habib, sons of 'Amr. One sneered that if Allah had sent him, he himself would tear down the cover of the Ka'bah. The second asked whether Allah could find no one else to send. The third said he would never speak to him: if he was a messenger, he was too great to answer, and if he was lying, he was not worth it. He rose and asked them at least to keep his visit quiet.

For ten days he went from one leading man to the next, and none would hear him. Then they set their slaves and the town's fools on him. They lined the lanes and stoned him as he went, until blood ran down both his legs. Zaid, shielding him, was cut on the head. They drove him two or three miles out of the town, until he sank down to rest against the wall of an orchard.

## taif-prayer
title: The Prayer at the Orchard Wall

Tired and wounded, with the whole world against him, he turned to his Lord in words that have been kept to this day.

He complained to Allah of his weakness, his lack of means, and how small he was in people's eyes. He called on Him as the Most Merciful of the merciful, the Lord of the weak, and asked into whose hands He was leaving him: a stranger who would frown at him, or an enemy given power over his affairs? But if Allah was not angry with him, he did not care. Allah's well-being was wider for him. He sought refuge in the light of Allah's face, by which darkness is lit and the affairs of this world and the next are set right, from Allah's anger falling on him.
quote: If You are not angry with me, I do not care. — The Prophet ﷺ, at Ta'if

The orchard belonged to 'Utbah and Shaibah, the sons of Rabi'ah, chiefs of Makkah. Moved by kinship, they sent their Christian servant 'Addas to him with a plate of grapes. He took them, saying, "In the name of Allah."

'Addas was struck by this: the people of that land did not speak like that. The Prophet ﷺ asked where he was from, and he said Nineveh. "The town of the righteous man Yunus, son of Matta," the Prophet ﷺ said. "What do you know of Yunus?" asked 'Addas. "He is my brother. He was a prophet, and I am a prophet."

'Addas bent and kissed his head, his hands and his feet. When he came back, his masters asked what had come over him. "My masters, there is nothing on earth better than this man," he said. "He told me something only a prophet would know."

## jinn

On the way back he stopped for some days in the valley of Nakhlah. There, as he recited the Qur'an at night, a company of jinn listened to him. They told each other to be silent, and went back to their people as warners [46:29-31]. Surat Al-Jinn tells it too: "We have heard a wondrous recitation" [72:1-2].

He did not know they were there until the verses were revealed. It was a sign of Allah's support: no power in the earth could stop what He had decreed. His grief lifted, and he turned back toward Makkah with new resolve.

Zaid asked how they could go back into Makkah when its people had driven him out. "Zaid, Allah will make a way out of what you see," he said, "and Allah will help His religion and His Prophet."

Near Makkah he waited in the cave of Hira' and sent a man of Khuza'ah to ask for a protector. Al-Akhnas bin Shuraiq said he was an ally of Quraish and could not. Suhail bin 'Amr also refused. As the pilgrimage drew near, he went back to what he had done since the fourth year, presenting himself and his message to the tribes and to men one by one. Az-Zuhri names the tribes he went to, and none of them answered.

He told Banu 'Abdullah of Kalb that Allah had given their father a good name, but they would not hear him. None of the Arabs turned him away more harshly than Banu Hanifah. A man of Banu 'Amir bin Sa'sa'ah named Bahirah bin Firas saw his chance. If they backed him and he won, would they rule after him? "The matter belongs to Allah," he said. "He gives it to whom He wills." "Then you want us to offer our necks to the Arabs for you, and give the rule to others when you win?" They refused. Back home, an old man of their tribe who had been too old to travel heard what had happened. He put his hand to his head and said that this was a true descendant of Isma'il, and asked how they could have let him go.

He met the tribes by night, so Makkah could not stand in his way. One night at Al-'Aqabah in Mina, he heard men talking and found six men of the Khazraj of Yathrib. He sat with them, explained Islam and recited the Qur'an.

The men of Yathrib had long heard their Jewish neighbours say that a prophet was soon to come, and that with him they would destroy their enemies as 'Ad had been destroyed. "This is the prophet they threaten us with," the six said to one another. "Let them not get to him before us." They became Muslims and told him that no people were more torn by hatred than theirs. Perhaps Allah would unite them through him. They went home and spoke of him, and soon there was hardly a house in Yathrib where he was not talked of.

## isra
title: The Night Journey
word: Al-Aqsa

y: 620.7
place: From Makkah to Jerusalem

Scholars differ on its date. Some place it in the first year of his prophethood, some in the fifth, and some on the 27th of Rajab of the tenth. Others say sixteen months before the Hijrah, fourteen months before it, or one year before it. Khadijah died before the five prayers were made obligatory, and they were made obligatory that night, so the first three cannot be right. Surat Al-Isra' suggests a late date. The book inclines to a time before the first pledge of 'Aqabah, or between the two pledges.

Ibn Al-Qayyim sums it up. He was carried, in body as well as spirit, from the Sacred Mosque in Makkah to Jerusalem, riding Al-Buraq with Jibreel. There he tied it at the gate of the mosque and led the prophets in prayer [17:1].

- First year of prophethood: At-Tabari's view
- Fifth year: preferred by An-Nawawi and Al-Qurtubi
- 27 Rajab, tenth year: Al-Mansurpuri's view
- Ramadan, twelfth year: sixteen months before the Hijrah
- Muharram, thirteenth year: fourteen months before the Hijrah
- Rabi' al-Awwal, thirteenth year: a year before the Hijrah

## miraj
title: Through the Seven Heavens
word: Mi'raj

From Jerusalem he was taken up through the heavens. At each, Jibreel asked for it to be opened, and in each a prophet welcomed him, returned his greeting and affirmed his prophethood. When he left Musa, Musa wept: a man sent after him would lead more of his followers into Paradise than he had.

He was taken to Sidrat Al-Muntaha, the lote tree of the furthest boundary, and shown Al-Bait Al-Ma'mur. Seventy thousand angels go round it every day, never to return. Then he was brought near, and Allah revealed to His servant what He revealed. Fifty prayers were made obligatory.

Coming down, he met Musa, who sent him back to ask for less. Again and again he went back, until the prayers were five. "I am ashamed to ask my Lord again," he said. "I accept." Then a voice called: "I have decreed My obligation and lightened it for My servants." Whether he saw Allah with his eyes, the book notes, is not established from any of the Companions. The drawing near in Surat An-Najm [53:8] is Jibreel's.
list:
- The first heaven: Adam; the souls of the blessed on his right, the wretched on his left
- The second: Yahya (John) and 'Isa (Jesus)
- The third: Yusuf (Joseph)
- The fourth: Idris
- The fifth: Harun (Aaron)
- The sixth: Musa (Moses)
- The seventh: Ibrahim (Abraham)

## siddiq

When he told of it, Quraish mocked and pressed him with questions. They asked him to describe the mosque in Jerusalem, a place he had never been to, and he described it exactly. He told them of their caravans on the road, of camels gone astray, and of water he had drunk from a covered vessel while its owners slept. It only drove them further from the truth.

They went to Abu Bakr, sure that this would turn him away. He said that he believed it. From that day he was called As-Siddiq, the one who confirms the truth.

The journey was "to show him of Our signs" [17:1], as Ibrahim was shown the kingdom of the heavens and the earth to be among the certain [6:75], and as Musa was shown "of Our greater signs" [20:23]. The book reads in it the passing of leadership to a new people, holding both mosques of Ibrahim's faith.
quote: Yes, I believe it. — Abu Bakr, as the book tells it

With the twelve he sent Mus'ab bin 'Umair, the first envoy of Islam, to teach them the Qur'an and the religion. He stayed with As'ad bin Zurarah, and Islam spread from house to house and clan to clan.

One day they sat by a well in the quarter of Banu Zafar. Usaid bin Hudair, a chief of Banu 'Abdul-Ashhal, came at them with his spear, cursing. "Sit and listen," said Mus'ab. "If you like it, accept it. If not, we will stop." "That is fair," said Usaid. He planted his spear in the sand, listened, heard the Qur'an, and his face lit up before he said a word. He washed, bore witness and prayed.

Then he sent Sa'd bin Mu'adh, his clan's chief, to them, and Sa'd too believed. Sa'd went back and told his people he would not speak to them until they believed. By evening, every man and woman of 'Abdul-Ashhal was Muslim except one, Al-'Usairim. He held back until the day of Uhud, then believed, fought and was killed before he had made a single prostration. "He did little and was rewarded much," the Prophet ﷺ said.

Before the next pilgrimage, Mus'ab went back to Makkah with the news: Islam had reached every house of the Helpers but one.
