## ten-years
title: Ten Years in Madinah
word: Ten
mood: oasis
when: 1 – 11 AH · 622 – 632 CE
y: 622.76
place: Madinah
look: list
tags: madinah
The ten years in Madinah fall into three stages. The first was the hardest: trouble and conflict inside the city, and wave after wave from outside meant to wipe the new faith out. The second was a truce with the leaders of Quraish, in which the Prophet ﷺ wrote to the kings beyond Arabia. In the third, people came into Islam in crowds, and delegations from every corner of Arabia arrived in Madinah to give him their pledge.

The Hijrah had not been only an escape from Makkah's torment. It was a move to a safe place where a new society could be built, and every Muslim able to help was bound to help build it, with the Messenger ﷺ as its leader in everything.
list:
- The first stage: conflict within and attack from without; it ended with the Treaty of Al-Hudaibiyah, Dhul-Qa'dah 6 AH
- The second stage: a truce with Quraish, and letters to kings; it ended with the conquest of Makkah, Ramadan 8 AH
- The third stage: people entering Islam in crowds, and delegations from all Arabia; it ended with his death, Rabi' al-Awwal 11 AH

## three-peoples
title: Three Peoples in One City
word: Yathrib
mood: oasis
when: On his arrival, 1 AH
y: 622.77
place: Madinah
look: list
tags: madinah, society, people
In Makkah the believers had shared one aim but had been scattered and powerless. In Madinah the whole of their life was in their own hands: trade and money, government, war and peace, what was lawful and what was not. Allah gave the laws, and the Prophet ﷺ explained them, put them into practice and trained people by them [62:2]. A society like that could not be built in a month or a year.

He had three peoples to deal with, each with its own problems. Among the believers, the Helpers had land and homes but carried the old feuds of the Aws and Khazraj, while the Emigrants arrived homeless and penniless, more of them every month, as Quraish's boycott squeezed the city's trade. Among those who had not believed, some soon did; others hated the new faith but dared not show it. And there were the Jews, who had settled in the Hijaz long before.
list:
- The Companions: the Helpers, settled but divided by old feuds; the Emigrants, homeless and growing in number
- Madinah's idolaters: 'Abdullah bin Ubai had been about to be crowned over both the Aws and the Khazraj; robbed of it, he professed Islam and led the hypocrites
- The Jews: Banu Qainuqa', allies of the Khazraj, and Banu An-Nadir and Banu Quraizah, allies of the Aws; they held much of the city's trade and lent at interest

## is-it-he
title: "Is It Really He?"
word: Is it he?
mood: dusk
when: At Quba', 1 AH
y: 622.78
place: Quba' and Madinah
look: quote
tags: madinah, people
Safiyah, the daughter of Huyai bin Akhtab, later a Mother of the Believers, remembered the day he reached Quba'. She was her father's and her uncle Abu Yasir's favourite child; whenever they came home they would make a fuss of her above all the others. That day they went to see the Prophet ﷺ and came back at sunset, dragging their feet. She ran to them smiling, and neither of them even turned to her. She heard her uncle ask her father whether it was really he. It was, he said, by Allah. Had he truly recognised him? Yes, and his heart burned with enmity towards him.

'Abdullah bin Salam, the most learned of their rabbis, came the first day with questions to test whether he was a Prophet, heard the answers and became a Muslim. He knew what his people would say, so the Prophet ﷺ called some of them and asked about him first. They praised his learning and his standing. When they learned he had become a Muslim, they called him the worst of them all.
quote: My heart is burning with enmity towards him. — Abu Yasir bin Akhtab, as his niece Safiyah heard it

## masjid
title: The Mosque
word: Masjid
mood: sand
when: 1 AH
y: 622.8
place: Where his camel knelt, Madinah
look: list
tags: madinah, faith
His first work was a mosque, on the very spot where his camel had knelt. The ground belonged to two orphan boys, and it was bought from them. It held some old graves of idolaters, which were dug up, and palm trees, which were cut down; the ground was cleared and levelled. He carried the mud bricks and stones himself with the others, and as they worked they sang that there is no life but the life of the Hereafter, and asked Allah to forgive the Helpers and the Emigrants.

Beside it were built rooms for his family, of stone and mud brick roofed with palm leaves. A place was kept at the back for believers who had no home and no family. The call to prayer began in these days, after the dream of 'Abdullah bin Zaid. The mosque was never only a place of prayer. It was a school where Islam was taught, a hall where old enemies sat down together, and the seat from which every affair of the Muslims was run.
list:
- Its qiblah: towards Jerusalem, at first
- Its building: mud brick and stone, two beams holding up its roof
- Its size: about a hundred yards a side, with doors on three sides
- The place at its back: for the poor who had neither family nor home

## brotherhood
title: Brothers
word: Brothers
mood: oasis
when: 1 AH
y: 622.82
place: The house of Anas bin Malik, Madinah
look: quote
tags: madinah, companions, society
Next he made the believers brothers. In the house of Anas bin Malik, ninety men gathered, half of them Emigrants and half Helpers, and he paired them, each Emigrant with a Helper. It was not a word but a bond binding blood and wealth: at first a man inherited from his brother in faith, until the ayah came at the time of Badr that kin by blood are closer in inheritance [8:75].

Sa'd bin Ar-Rabi' said to the man he was paired with, 'Abdur-Rahman bin 'Awf, "I am the richest of the Helpers. Half my wealth is yours, and I have two wives; choose one, I will divorce her, and when her waiting is done you can marry her." 'Abdur-Rahman prayed for blessings on him, and asked only to be shown the way to the market. He traded, prospered, and was soon married.

The Helpers asked the Prophet ﷺ to divide their palm groves between them and their brothers. He would not lay that on them; instead the Emigrants worked the groves with them, and the harvest was shared. They took only what they needed to live.
quote: Show me the way to the market. — 'Abdur-Rahman bin 'Awf, offered half of his brother's wealth

## charter
title: The Charter of Madinah
word: Charter
mood: parchment
when: 1 AH
y: 622.84
place: Madinah
look: list
tags: madinah, treaties, society
He then wrote a charter between the Emigrants, the Helpers and those who joined them, so that nothing of the old tribal law could creep back. The believers were one nation, before all other people. The clans kept their old duties of paying blood money and ransoming their own, but under a new rule: no believer would be left alone under a debt, and none would side with a wrongdoer, even his own son.

With the Jews of Madinah he made a treaty of the same kind, which left them free in their religion and their wealth. He meant no exile, no seizing of land, no war. Madinah and its villages became one state, its capital Madinah, its head the Prophet ﷺ.
list:
- One nation: the believers are one community, apart from all others
- No one left under a debt: they pay blood money and ransom their captives together
- Against the wrongdoer: every believer's hand is against the one who sows strife, even his own son
- The weakest's protection: the protection of Allah is one; the humblest believer may give it
- No shelter for a criminal: whoever shelters or helps one bears Allah's curse
- The Jews of Banu 'Awf: one community with the believers, each with its own religion; each helps the other if attacked
- Quraish: those under it boycott Quraish and give them no help
- Disputes: whatever they differ in goes to Allah and to Muhammad ﷺ

## teaching
title: What He Taught Them
word: Peace
mood: white
when: The first years in Madinah
y: 622.86
place: Madinah
look: list
tags: society, character
On these foundations he built people. He taught them to purify their souls, to keep good manners and good relations, to worship, and above all to obey Allah and His Messenger. He would remind them of the reward of each deed and tie what he asked of them to the Revelation.

'Abdullah bin Salam said that when he first saw the Prophet's face he knew it was not the face of a liar, and the first words he heard from him were these: spread the greeting of peace, feed people, keep the ties of kinship, and pray at night while others sleep, and you will enter Paradise in peace. Ibn Mas'ud said of the Companions raised this way that they were the best of this nation, the purest in heart, the deepest in knowledge and the least given to show.
list:
- Feed people, and greet those you know and those you do not
- None of you truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself
- The Muslim is the one from whose tongue and hand the Muslims are safe
- He will not enter Paradise whose neighbour is not safe from his harm
- The believers in their love for each other are like one body: when one part aches, the whole body feels it
- Be merciful to those on earth, and the One in the heaven will be merciful to you
- Charity puts out sin as water puts out fire
- Guard yourselves from the Fire even with half a date; and if you cannot, with a good word

## threats
title: Quraish Write to Ibn Ubai
word: Ultimatum
mood: ember
when: 1 – 2 AH
y: 622.9
place: Madinah and Makkah
look: quote
tags: madinah, makkah
Quraish kept watch over the Muslims left in Makkah and tormented them. To 'Abdullah bin Ubai and the idolaters of Madinah they sent an ultimatum: fight Muhammad or drive him out, or they would come in force, kill their fighting men and take their women. Ibn Ubai's men gathered to fight. The Prophet ﷺ went to them and asked if they would really fight their own sons and brothers, and they broke up. Ibn Ubai went along outwardly, and went on plotting.

Sa'd bin Mu'adh went to Makkah for 'Umrah and stayed with Umaiyah bin Khalaf, who took him round the Ka'bah at noon when it was empty. Abu Jahl found them and told Sa'd that had he not been under Umaiyah's protection he would not have gone home alive. Sa'd answered that if Abu Jahl stopped him from this, he would stop Abu Jahl from something dearer to him: his road through Madinah.

The danger was real. Guards stood watch around the Prophet's house. One night he wished aloud for a righteous man to keep watch over him, and there was a clatter of weapons outside: it was Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas, come to guard him. So it went on until the ayah came that Allah would protect him from the people [5:67], and he put his head out and sent his guards home.
quote: O people, go away, for Allah has protected me. — The Prophet ﷺ, when the ayah came

## leave-to-fight
title: Leave to Fight
word: Leave
mood: iron
when: After the Hijrah, its date not certain
y: 622.95
place: Madinah
look: quote
tags: battles, revelation
In Makkah the believers had been told to be patient. Now, in Madinah, with Quraish set on destroying them, Allah gave them leave to fight [22:39-40]: leave for those who were fought against, because they had been wronged. It came with its purpose: those who, if given power in the land, establish prayer, give zakah, enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong [22:41]. That it came after the Hijrah is certain; its exact date is not.

The wisest course was to take control of the trade road from Makkah to Syria, which ran close to Madinah. There were two ways to do it. One was treaties with the tribes along the road and between it and Madinah; the charter and the pacts with the neighbouring tribes had begun that. The other was to send armed patrols along the road, to show the Jews, the Bedouin and Quraish that the believers were no longer afraid, and that Quraish's trade would not pass in peace while they persecuted the believers left behind in Makkah.
quote: Permission is given to those who are fought against, because they have been wronged; and Allah is able to give them victory. — Al-Hajj 22:39

## patrols
title: The First Patrols
word: Patrols
mood: dusk
when: Ramadan 1 AH – Jumada 2 AH · 623 CE
y: 623.2
place: The road between Makkah and Syria
look: list
tags: battles
Eight such marches came before Badr, four sent out and four led by the Prophet ﷺ himself. Their flags were white. In the first seven no one was killed and nothing was plundered; the only raid on property was Kurz bin Jabir Al-Fihri's, on Madinah's pastures. Quraish learned that the Muslims now watched three hundred miles of their road.

On the march to Waddan he made a pact with Banu Damrah: they were safe in their lives and wealth, and would help him when he called on them. On the march to Dhul-'Ushairah he made one with Banu Mudlij. It was there that he had set out to catch the caravan returning from Syria, the caravan that would lead to Badr.
list:
- Saif Al-Bahr, Ramadan 1 AH: Hamzah with thirty Emigrants met Abu Jahl's caravan of three hundred; Majdi bin 'Amr, friend to both sides, kept them apart; the first flag, carried by Abu Marthad Kannaz
- Rabigh, Shawwal 1 AH: 'Ubaidah bin Al-Harith with sixty riders met Abu Sufyan with two hundred; a few arrows flew; Al-Miqdad and 'Utbah bin Ghazwan crossed over from Quraish to the Muslims
- Al-Kharrar, Dhul-Qa'dah 1 AH: Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas with twenty riders, five days out; the caravan had passed the day before
- Waddan (Al-Abwa'), Safar 2 AH: the first he led himself, seventy men, fifteen days; a pact with Banu Damrah
- Buwat, Rabi' al-Awwal 2 AH: two hundred men after Umaiyah bin Khalaf's caravan of 2,500 camels; it had gone
- Safwan, Rabi' al-Awwal 2 AH: after Kurz bin Jabir, who had raided Madinah's pastures; he escaped; it was called the first Badr
- Dhul-'Ushairah, Jumada 2 AH: up to two hundred men on thirty camels, turn by turn; the Syria caravan had passed days before; a pact with Banu Mudlij

## nakhlah
title: The Letter Opened After Two Days
word: Nakhlah
mood: dusk
when: Rajab 2 AH · January 624 CE
y: 624
place: Nakhlah, between Makkah and Ta'if
look: map patrols
tags: battles, revelation
He sent 'Abdullah bin Jahsh with twelve Emigrants and a sealed letter, not to be opened for two days. When he opened it, it told him to go on to Nakhlah, between Makkah and Ta'if, watch for a caravan of Quraish and bring back news. He read it to his men, and every one of them went on with him.

A caravan came by with raisins, food and other goods, 'Amr bin Al-Hadrami among its men. It was Rajab, a sacred month. They argued among themselves, and decided to attack. 'Amr bin Al-Hadrami was killed by an arrow, two men were taken and one escaped. They brought back the caravan and set aside a fifth of it.

The Prophet ﷺ was displeased that they had fought in the sacred month, and touched neither the goods nor the captives. Quraish made the most of it: Muhammad had broken the sacred months. Then the ayah came [2:217]: fighting in it is grave, but graver still with Allah is to bar people from His way, to disbelieve in Him, to keep them from the Sacred Mosque and drive out its people, and persecution is worse than killing. The two captives were later freed and blood money paid for the man killed.
facts:
- Men: 'Abdullah bin Jahsh and twelve Emigrants, six camels
- The order: a letter, to be read after two days on the road
- The month: Rajab, a sacred month
- The answer: Al-Baqarah 2:217

## qiblah
title: Facing the Ka'bah
word: Qiblah
mood: dawn
when: Sha'ban 2 AH · February 624 CE
y: 624.1
place: Madinah
look: quote
tags: revelation, faith, madinah
In Sha'ban of the second year the command to fight came plainly [2:190-193]: fight in Allah's way those who fight you, and do not transgress. More ayahs followed teaching them how to fight and how to treat captives [47:4-7], and rebuking the hypocrites who looked at him like a man fainting at death when fighting was mentioned [47:20].

In the same month came the command to turn the qiblah from Jerusalem to the Sacred Mosque in Makkah [2:144]. It sifted the ranks: hypocrites, and the weak of faith among the Jews and others, showed what they were, and the Muslims were rid of them. And it pointed to a task ahead. The believers' qiblah could not be left in the hands of idolaters for ever.
quote: So turn your face towards the Sacred Mosque; and wherever you are, turn your faces towards it. — Al-Baqarah 2:144
