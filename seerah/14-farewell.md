The call was complete and a new society stood on "there is no god but Allah." Something in him told him his time in the world was ending. When he sent Mu'adh bin Jabal to Yemen in the tenth year, he told him he might not see him again; he might pass by this mosque, and his grave. Mu'adh wept.

That Ramadan he spent twenty days in i'tikaf in the mosque, where he used to spend ten, and Jibreel went through the Qur'an with him twice, where he used to go through it once.

## hajj
title: The Farewell Pilgrimage
word: Labbaik
mood: white
when: End of Dhul-Qa'dah – 4 Dhul-Hijjah 10 AH · 632 CE
y: 632.15
place: From Madinah to Makkah
look: quote
tags: faith, journeys, makkah
He let it be known he would make the Hajj, and people poured into Madinah from everywhere to make it with him. On a Saturday, with four days of Dhul-Qa'dah left, he combed his hair, put on perfume and his garments, saddled his camel, and set out after noon. He reached Dhul-Hulaifah before 'Asr, prayed two rak'ahs and spent the night. In the morning he told them that one had come to him in the night from his Lord, and said: pray in this blessed valley, and say, 'Umrah together with Hajj.

He bathed for ihram, and 'Aishah perfumed his head and body with her own hands, with dharirah and a perfume with musk in it; its traces could be seen in his hair and beard. He put on his ihram, prayed Dhuhr as two rak'ahs, declared his Hajj joined with 'Umrah, and cried, "Labbaik, Allahumma, labbaik," and again on his camel, and again on the open plain. He spent the night at Dhu Tuwa, and on Sunday morning, the fourth of Dhul-Hijjah, entered Makkah: eight days on the road. He went round the Ka'bah and between As-Safa and Al-Marwah, and stayed in ihram, for he had brought his sacrificial animals. He camped above the town at Al-Hajun.

Those who had no animals with them he told to make it an 'Umrah and leave ihram. They hesitated, and he said that had he known then what he knew now, he would not have brought his animals, and would have left ihram himself. Then they did it.
quote: Here I am, O Allah, here I am, at Your service. — The Prophet ﷺ, setting out for the Farewell Hajj

On the eighth day he went to Mina and prayed there five prayers, and after sunrise on the ninth rode to 'Arafah, where a tent had been pitched for him at Namirah. When the sun passed its height he had Al-Qaswa' saddled, and went down into the valley. A hundred and twenty-four thousand people, or a hundred and forty-four thousand, were gathered round him, and he spoke to them, Rabi'ah bin Umaiyah bin Khalaf calling out his words after him so all could hear.

"O people, listen to me, for I do not know whether I will meet you in this place after this year." At the end he asked them what they would say when they were asked about him. "We bear witness that you have conveyed the message and fulfilled your mission." He raised his forefinger to the sky and pointed it down at the people: "O Allah, bear witness. O Allah, bear witness. O Allah, bear witness."
list:
- Your blood, your wealth and your honour are as sacred to each other as this day, in this month, in this land
- Everything of the Ignorance is under my feet; its blood feuds are void, the first I void being the blood of Ibn Rabi'ah bin Al-Harith
- Its usury is void, the first I void being that owed to my uncle Al-'Abbas
- Fear Allah concerning your women: you took them on Allah's trust; they owe you faithfulness, and you owe them food and clothing in fairness
- I have left among you something which, if you hold fast to it, you will never go astray: the Book of Allah

When he finished speaking, the ayah came down: This day I have perfected your religion for you, completed My favour upon you, and chosen Islam for you as your religion [5:3]. 'Umar wept. Why? "After perfection there is nothing but decline."

Bilal called the iqamah, and he prayed Dhuhr, and then at once 'Asr. He rode to the place of standing, faced the qiblah and stood until the sun had set and its yellow faded. With Usamah up behind him he went on to Muzdalifah, prayed Maghrib and 'Isha' with one call and two iqamahs, and lay down until dawn. After Fajr he rode to Al-Mash'ar Al-Haram and stood facing the qiblah, praising Allah until it was full light, then went on to Mina before sunrise, Al-Fadl bin Al-'Abbas behind him now, hurrying through the valley of Muhassir.

He threw seven small pebbles at the great Jamrah, saying "Allahu akbar" with each. At the place of sacrifice he slaughtered sixty-three camels with his own hand and gave 'Ali the rest to slaughter, a hundred in all; a piece of each was cooked in one pot, and they ate of it and drank its broth. ## day-of-sacrifice
title: The Day of Sacrifice
word: Convey
mood: white
when: 10 – 13 Dhul-Hijjah 10 AH
y: 632.19
place: Mina, Al-Abtah and the Ka'bah
look: quote
tags: faith, society
Late in the morning of the Day of Sacrifice he spoke again, from a grey mule, 'Ali calling out his words. Time had come round to the shape it had on the day Allah created the heavens and the earth: twelve months, four of them sacred. What month was this? Allah and His Messenger knew best; he was silent so long they thought he would rename it. "Is it not Dhul-Hijjah?" What land? "Is it not the Sacred Land?" What day? "Is it not the Day of Sacrifice?" Then your blood, wealth and honour are as sacred to each other as this day, in this month, in this land. You will meet your Lord and He will ask you about your deeds; so do not go astray after me, killing one another. "Have I conveyed?" "Yes." "O Allah, bear witness."

A wrongdoer wrongs only himself; no man is to be blamed for what his father or his son did. Satan had despaired of ever being worshipped in this land, but he would be obeyed in things they thought small, and that would please him.

He spent the days of Tashriq at Mina, teaching. Surat An-Nasr came down in them, and he spoke again on the middle day. On the thirteenth he left Mina for the hillside of Banu Kinanah at Al-Abtah, prayed the four prayers there, slept a little, then rode to the Ka'bah for the farewell tawaf, and set out for Madinah: not to rest, but to go on striving.
quote: Let those who are here tell those who are not; perhaps the one told will understand it better than the one who heard it. — The Prophet ﷺ, at Mina

## usamah
title: Usamah's Army
word: Usamah
mood: iron
when: Safar 11 AH · 632 CE
y: 632.37
place: Madinah, and Al-Jurf
look: quote
tags: battles, companions, family
Rome would not let the Muslims be: it had even put to death its own governor at Ma'an, Farwah bin 'Amr, for becoming a Muslim. In Safar of the eleventh year he raised a great army and gave it to Usamah, son of Zaid bin Harithah, to ride into the borderlands of Al-Balqa' and Ad-Darum in Palestine, to make Rome afraid and give heart to the Arabs on its borders.

People talked: Usamah was too young to command. He answered them, and men came to join Usamah in great numbers. The army marched out and camped at Al-Jurf, three miles from Madinah. Then came the worrying news of his illness, and it waited, to see what Allah would decree. It was the first army Abu Bakr sent as caliph.
quote: If you question his command, you questioned his father's before him. By Allah, his father was fit to command and was among the dearest of people to me; and this son of his is among the dearest of people to me after him. — The Prophet ﷺ, of Usamah

## farewells
title: Farewells
word: Al-Baqi'
mood: night
when: Early Safar 11 AH
y: 632.38
place: Uhud, the mosque, and Al-Baqi'
look: quote
tags: companions, signs
Surat An-Nasr had told him that his time was near. Early in Safar he went to Uhud and prayed over its martyrs, as if bidding farewell to the living and the dead. Then he climbed the pulpit: he was going ahead of them, and would be a witness over them; by Allah, they would soon meet him at the Pool; he had been given the keys of the treasures of the earth; he did not fear that they would go back to worshipping others after him, but that they would compete for this world.

One midnight he went out to Al-Baqi' and asked forgiveness for the dead there. Trials were coming, he told them, like pieces of a dark night, each following the other, each worse than the last.

## illness
title: The Illness
word: Fever
mood: grief
when: Monday 29 Safar 11 AH · May 632 CE
y: 632.4

tags: family
On Monday the twenty-ninth of Safar he went to a funeral at Al-Baqi'. On the way back his head began to ache, and the fever rose so high that its heat could be felt through the cloth on his head. He was ill thirteen or fourteen days, and led the prayers for eleven of them.

As the illness grew heavy, he would ask his wives, "Where am I tomorrow? Where am I tomorrow?" They understood, and gave him leave to stay where he wished. He went to 'Aishah's room leaning on Al-Fadl bin Al-'Abbas and 'Ali, his head bound, his feet dragging on the ground, and spent his last week there. 'Aishah would recite the two surahs of refuge and the prayers he had taught, blow into her hands, and pass them over him for their blessing.
quote: Where am I tomorrow? Where am I tomorrow? — The Prophet ﷺ, in his last illness

## five-days
title: Five Days Before
word: Abu Bakr
mood: grief
when: Wednesday, five days before his death
y: 632.42
place: The pulpit of his mosque
look: quote
tags: companions, family, character
On Wednesday the fever burned and he fainted from the pain. "Pour seven skins of water over me, so I can go out to the people." They sat him in a tub and poured until he said, "Enough, enough." Then he went into the mosque with his head bound, sat on the pulpit and spoke. Allah's curse was on the Jews and Christians who made their prophets' graves places of worship; they were not to make his grave an idol to be worshipped. If he had ever struck a man's back, here was his back; if he had ever wronged a man's honour, here was his honour. After Dhuhr he spoke again. A man said he owed him three dirhams. "Pay him, Fadl."

He told them to be good to the Helpers: they were his family, and with them he had found shelter; they had done what was on them, and what was owed them remained; acknowledge the good they had done and pass over their faults. Then he said that Allah had given a servant of His the choice between whatever he wished of this world and what is with Him, and the servant had chosen what is with Him. Abu Bakr wept: "Our fathers and mothers be your ransom!" People wondered at the old man, until they understood that the servant was the Prophet ﷺ himself, and that Abu Bakr was the most knowing of them.
quote: No one has done more for me with his company and his wealth than Abu Bakr. If I were to take a bosom friend other than my Lord, I would take Abu Bakr; but his is the brotherhood of Islam. Let every door into the mosque be shut, but Abu Bakr's. — The Prophet ﷺ

On Thursday, in great pain, he said, "Come, let me write something for you after which you will never go astray." 'Umar said that the Prophet ﷺ was in great pain, and they had the Qur'an: the Book of Allah was enough for them. Others wanted it written, and they argued, until he told them to leave him.

He led every prayer until that Thursday's Maghrib, in which he recited Surat Al-Mursalat. At 'Isha' he asked if the people had prayed. "No; they are waiting for you." He asked for water, washed, tried to rise, and fainted. When he came to, he asked again, and again, three times. Then he sent word for Abu Bakr to lead them. 'Aishah begged him three or four times to excuse her soft-hearted father; "You are like the women of Yusuf's story," he said. "Tell Abu Bakr to lead the people." Abu Bakr led seventeen prayers in his lifetime.

## curtain
title: A Smile Through the Curtain
word: Smile
mood: dawn
when: Saturday or Sunday, and Monday dawn, 12 Rabi' al-Awwal 11 AH
y: 632.43
place: The mosque, and 'Aishah's room
look: quote

On Saturday or Sunday he felt a little better, and came out for Dhuhr leaning on two men. Abu Bakr, leading, began to step back; he signed to him to stay, and had himself seated at his left, and led the prayer, Abu Bakr repeating his "Allahu akbar" so the people could hear.

On Sunday he freed his slaves, gave away the seven dinars he had in charity, and gave his weapons to the Muslims. That night 'Aishah had to borrow oil from a neighbour for her lamp. His coat of mail lay in pawn with a Jew for thirty measures of barley.

At Fajr on Monday, as Abu Bakr led the prayer, they saw the curtain of 'Aishah's room lift. He looked at them standing in their rows, and smiled with joy. Abu Bakr stepped back to the row, thinking he was coming out. The Muslims were so glad they were almost distracted from their prayer. He signed to them to finish it, went back in, and let the curtain fall.

That morning he called Fatimah and whispered to her, and she wept; then whispered again, and she laughed. After his death 'Aishah asked her why. The first time, she said, he had told her he would not recover from this illness, and she wept; then that she would be the first of his family to follow him, and she laughed. He told her too that she would be the lady of the women of the world.

Seeing his pain, Fatimah cried out at how much her father suffered. "There will be no more pain for your father after today." He asked for Al-Hasan and Al-Husain and kissed them, and told those around him to care for them, and called his wives and counselled them. The pain grew so sharp that he told 'Aishah he still felt the food he had tasted at Khaibar. Again and again he told them to keep the prayer, and to be good to their slaves.

'Aishah held him against her. Of Allah's favours to her, she said, was that he died in her house, between her chest and her throat, and that Allah mixed her saliva with his at the end. Her brother 'Abdur-Rahman came in with a siwak in his hand, and she saw him looking at it, and knew he wanted it. She took it for him and softened it, and he cleaned his teeth with it as well as he could.

There was a bowl of water by him. He dipped his hand in it and wiped his face: "There is no god but Allah. Death has its agonies." Then he raised his hand, or his finger, and looked up, and his lips moved. She listened, and heard him say: with those You have blessed, the prophets, the truthful, the martyrs and the righteous; O Allah, forgive me and have mercy on me, and join me to the Highest Companion. Three times he said it, the Highest Companion. Then his hand fell.

It was late in the morning of Monday the twelfth of Rabi' al-Awwal, in the eleventh year. He was sixty-three years and four days old.
quote: O Allah, the Highest Companion. — The last words of the Prophet ﷺ

## darkest-day
title: The Darkest Day
word: Muhammad is dead
mood: grief
when: Monday, 12 Rabi' al-Awwal 11 AH
y: 632.436
place: The Prophet's mosque
look: quote
tags: companions, revelation, family
The news ran through Madinah, and darkness fell on it. "I never saw a day better or brighter than the day he came to us," Anas said, "and I never saw a day uglier or darker than the day he died." Fatimah cried: O my father, who answered his Lord's call; O my father, whose home is the Garden of Paradise; O my father, to Jibreel we announce your death.

'Umar stood up before the people, beside himself. Some hypocrites, he said, were claiming the Messenger of Allah had died. He had not died; he had gone to his Lord as Musa did, and would come back, and cut off the hands and feet of those who said he was dead.

Abu Bakr rode in from his house at As-Sunh, spoke to no one, went straight into 'Aishah's room, uncovered the face beneath its Yemeni cloth, bent and kissed him, and wept. "My father and mother be your ransom. Allah will not make you taste death twice; the death written for you, you have tasted." He went out. "Sit down, 'Umar." 'Umar would not, and the people left him and turned to Abu Bakr. Then he recited the ayah, and it was as if they had never heard it before; everyone was reciting it. 'Umar said that when he heard it his legs gave way and he fell to the ground, and knew that the Prophet ﷺ was dead.
quote: Whoever worshipped Muhammad, Muhammad is dead. Whoever worships Allah, Allah is Ever-Living and never dies. "Muhammad is no more than a messenger; messengers have passed away before him. If he dies or is killed, will you turn back on your heels?" — Abu Bakr, reciting Al 'Imran 3:144

## buried
title: Buried Where He Died
word: Lahd
mood: grief
when: Tuesday – the night before Wednesday, 11 AH
y: 632.44
place: 'Aishah's room, Madinah
look: list
tags: family, companions
Even before he was washed, the Helpers and Emigrants argued over who should lead them, in the roofed hall of Banu Sa'idah, all through Monday into the night, until they gave their pledge to Abu Bakr. All that while his body lay on his bed under a cloth, the door locked.

On Tuesday he was washed in his clothes, three times, with water and lote leaves, from Ghars, the well of Sa'd bin Khaithamah at Quba' that he used to drink from, and shrouded in three white cotton sheets from Yemen, with no shirt and no turban. There was some disagreement over where to bury him, until Abu Bakr said he had heard him say that a prophet is buried where he dies. So Abu Talhah lifted the bed on which he had died and dug the grave beneath it, a lahd with a niche in its side.

They came into the room ten at a time and prayed over him, each alone, with no imam: first his own clan, then the Emigrants, then the Helpers, then the women, and last the children. It went on through Tuesday and long into the night. "We did not know he was being buried," 'Aishah said, "until we heard the sound of digging, in the depth of the night before Wednesday."
