## emigrants
title: The Believers Leave
word: Hijrah
mood: dusk
when: After the Great Pledge, 13th – 14th year of prophethood
y: 622.5
place: From Makkah to Yathrib
look: people
tags: journeys, companions
After the Great Pledge, he gave the believers leave to go to Yathrib. To leave meant giving up home and wealth, and the road itself was not safe. Quraish did all they could to stop them.

The first to go, a year before the Great Pledge, was Abu Salamah. His wife's people kept her back, and their little son was snatched from her, his arm wrenched out of joint. For a year Umm Salamah sat weeping each day, until her people relented and gave her back her son. She set out alone on the five-hundred-kilometre road. At At-Tan'im, 'Uthman bin Talhah met her and led her all the way to Quba', where her husband was.

Quraish told Suhaib Ar-Rumi he had come to them a pauper and grown rich among them, so he could not leave. He offered them all his wealth, and they let him go. "Suhaib has profited," the Prophet ﷺ said. Abu Jahl tricked 'Aiyash bin Abi Rabi'ah into coming home, telling him their mother had sworn never to comb her hair or seek shade until she saw him. On the way they tied him up and brought him back to Makkah. Within two months whole quarters of Makkah stood empty. Only the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr, 'Ali and those held captive remained.

He had told them he had been shown the place of their emigration in a dream: a land of date palms between two mountains. Abu Bakr was ready to go, but the Prophet ﷺ told him to wait, for he hoped to be given leave himself. So Abu Bakr waited, and fed two camels on the leaves of the samur tree for four months.
people:
- Abu Salamah: the first to emigrate, a year before the Great Pledge
- Umm Salamah: a year apart from husband and son, then the road alone
- Suhaib Ar-Rumi: bought his freedom to go with everything he owned
- 'Umar bin Al-Khattab: came to Madinah with twenty of the Companions
- 'Aiyash bin Abi Rabi'ah: tricked back to Makkah and held there

## nadwah
title: The Council of Quraish
word: Nadwah
mood: ember
when: Thursday, 26 Safar, 14th year of prophethood · 12 September 622 CE
y: 622.7
place: Dar An-Nadwah, Makkah
look: list
tags: makkah
Yathrib's fighting men were known throughout Arabia. The city lay across the trade road to Syria, and Makkah's trade along it was worth around a quarter of a million gold dinars a year. If Muhammad ﷺ reached it, Quraish were in danger. Their chiefs gathered in Dar An-Nadwah: Abu Jahl, Jubair bin Mut'im, 'Utbah, Shaibah, Abu Sufyan, An-Nadr bin Al-Harith, Umaiyah bin Khalaf, Hakim bin Hizam and others.

It is reported that Iblis came to the door in the shape of an old man from Najd, asking to sit in on their counsel, and they let him in. They talked a long time, and Abu Jahl's plan won the day. Each clan would give one strong young man. Together they would strike him at once, so that his blood would be spread among all the clans and Banu 'Abd Manaf could never avenge it, only take blood money. They all agreed, and went to carry it out.
list:
- Banish him: rejected; his words would win the Arabs over, and they would come back against Makkah
- Imprison him for life: rejected; his followers would grow and set him free by force
- Kill him, with a young man from every clan: agreed, so that his blood could not be avenged

Jibreel told him of the plot, and of Allah's leave to go. 'Aishah tells it. They were sitting in Abu Bakr's house at noon, the hottest hour, when someone said, "Here is the Messenger of Allah, his head covered," at an hour he had never come before. "May my father and mother be his ransom," said Abu Bakr. "Something important brings him at this hour."

He asked that everyone else leave. "There is only your own family here," said Abu Bakr. "I have been given leave to emigrate," he said. "Your company, Messenger of Allah?" asked Abu Bakr. "Yes," he said. Then they made their plans, and he went home to wait for night.

## night
title: The Night of the Hijrah
word: Night
mood: night
when: Night of 27 Safar, 14th year of prophethood · 12 – 13 September 622 CE
y: 622.702
place: His house, Makkah
look: quote
tags: journeys, signs
Eleven men waited around his house through the night. Among them were Abu Jahl, Al-Hakam bin Abil-'As, 'Uqbah bin Abi Mu'ait, An-Nadr bin Al-Harith, Umaiyah bin Khalaf, Abu Lahab, Ubai bin Khalaf, and the brothers Nabih and Munbih bin Al-Hajjaj. Through a gap in the door they watched him lying in his bed, waiting for him to come out at dawn to pray. Abu Jahl walked among them, mocking his promises.

He told 'Ali to lie in his bed under his green cloak, assuring him no harm would come to him. Then he walked out between them, throwing a handful of dust over their heads as he recited: "And We have put a barrier before them and a barrier behind them, and covered them so that they do not see" [36:9]. He went to Abu Bakr's house, and before dawn they were gone.

In the morning the watchers found 'Ali rising from the bed. He said he did not know where he had gone.
quote: They plotted, and Allah planned, and Allah is the best of planners. — Al-Anfal 8:30

## thawr
title: The Cave of Thawr
word: Thawr
mood: night
when: 27 Safar – 1 Rabi' al-Awwal, 1 AH
y: 622.703
place: Mount Thawr, south of Makkah
look: map makkah
tags: journeys, signs
Knowing Quraish would search the road north to Yathrib, he took the road south, toward Yemen, which no one would expect. They walked about five miles to Mount Thawr, a rough, rocky mountain. His sandals were worn out, and it is said he walked on his toes to leave no trail. Abu Bakr carried him up to the cave.

Abu Bakr went in first to make sure it was safe. He cleaned it and stopped up its holes with strips torn from his own clothes. The Prophet ﷺ came in, laid his head in Abu Bakr's lap and slept. Something stung Abu Bakr's foot, and the pain made his tears fall on the Prophet's face. He woke, wiped his own saliva on the sting, and the pain went.

They stayed three nights: Friday, Saturday and Sunday. Each evening Abu Bakr's son 'Abdullah came with the news from Makkah and left before dawn to be seen among Quraish. Abu Bakr's shepherd, 'Amir bin Fuhairah, grazed his flock nearby and brought them milk at night.

## pursuit
title: A Hundred Camels
word: 100
mood: ember
when: 28 – 30 Safar, 1 AH
y: 622.704
place: Makkah and Mount Thawr
look: quote
tags: journeys, signs
refs: 9:40
Quraish dragged 'Ali to the Ka'bah, beat him and held him, but he told them nothing. They went to Abu Bakr's daughter Asma', and Abu Jahl struck her so hard her earring broke. They guarded every road out of Makkah and offered a hundred camels for each of the two men. Riders and trackers combed the country.

They came to the very mouth of the cave. "Prophet of Allah," whispered Abu Bakr, "if one of them looks down at his feet, he will see us." He was afraid for him, not for himself: if they killed Abu Bakr, it was one man, but if they killed him, the whole nation was lost. The answer is the one the Qur'an keeps: "Do not grieve; Allah is with us" [9:40].
quote: What do you think, Abu Bakr, of two when Allah is their third? — The Prophet ﷺ, reported by Al-Bukhari

## road
title: The Road by the Sea
word: Sea
mood: dusk
when: Rabi' al-Awwal, 1 AH · September 622 CE
y: 622.71
place: The coast road to Yathrib
look: map hijrah
tags: journeys
After three nights, when the search had slackened, their guide came to the cave as agreed with two of Abu Bakr's camels. He was 'Abdullah bin Uraiqit, not a Muslim, but trusted. Abu Bakr offered the better camel, and the Prophet ﷺ took it only on condition that he pay for it. Asma' brought their food and tied it up with half of her waistband. From then on she was called "the one with the two waistbands".

With 'Amir bin Fuhairah they set out on little-used tracks near the coast. Abu Bakr tells how they rode all that night and the next day until noon. Then he found a rock with some shade, spread a skin for the Prophet ﷺ to sleep, and kept watch. A shepherd boy came by, and Abu Bakr had him milk a ewe, cleaning its udder first. He cooled the milk with water and waited for the Prophet ﷺ to wake. "Drink, Messenger of Allah," he said, and he drank until Abu Bakr was content.

When anyone asked who the man with him was, Abu Bakr said, "A man who guides me on the way." They took him to mean the road. He meant the way of goodness.

## suraqah

Suraqah bin Malik heard that riders had been seen by the shore. He told the others it must be someone else, then quietly took his spear, slipped out the back of his house and rode after them for the reward.

As he drew near, his horse stumbled and threw him. He drew his divining arrows: the answer was the one he disliked. He rode on anyway. He could hear the Prophet ﷺ reciting, never turning round, while Abu Bakr kept looking back. Then his horse's forelegs sank into the ground to the knees, and when it pulled them free, dust rose like smoke. He knew then that this man would prevail. He called out to them that they were safe, told them everything Quraish had planned, and offered them provisions, which they refused. "Keep us hidden," was all the Prophet ﷺ asked. At Suraqah's request, 'Amir bin Fuhairah wrote him a promise of safety on a piece of leather.
quote: Do not grieve; Allah is with us. — The Prophet ﷺ to Abu Bakr, as Suraqah came [9:40]

## umm-mabad
title: Umm Ma'bad's Tent

y: 622.714
place: Qudaid
look: text

They came to two tents where a generous woman of Khuza'ah, Umm Ma'bad, sat at her door for any traveller who passed. It was a year of drought, and the flock was out at pasture. The only goat left was too weak and dry to go with it.

He asked her leave, passed his hand over its udder in Allah's name and prayed, and milk poured out. He gave her to drink first, then his companions, then drank himself. He milked again and left the vessel full for her.

Her husband came home with his thin goats and could not believe the milk. She described the blessed man who had passed, and her husband knew he was the one Quraish were hunting. Her description of him became one of the best-known in all the reports. Verses praising her guest were heard in Makkah from a voice no one could see. From them Asma' learned that they were headed for Madinah.

On the way he met Buraidah bin Al-Husaib of Aslam with about eighty men, who became Muslims and prayed the night prayer behind him. "Which people are you from?" he asked. "Aslam." "Then we are safe," he said to Abu Bakr: the name means peace. "And which clan?" "Sahm." "Then your victory has come." He liked good omens without trusting in signs.

Near Al-'Arj, Aws bin Hajar of Aslam found them with a slow camel and gave them his own, sending a boy, Mas'ud, to see them safely into Madinah. And they met Az-Zubair with a caravan of Muslims returning from Syria, who gave them white clothes.

## quba
title: Quba'
word: Quba'
mood: oasis
when: Monday, 8 Rabi' al-Awwal, 1 AH · 23 September 622 CE
y: 622.72
place: Quba', on the edge of Madinah
look: map madinah
tags: madinah, journeys
refs: 9:108
Since hearing he had left Makkah, the Muslims of Madinah had gone out each morning to the stony ground of the Harrah to wait, until the noon heat drove them home. One day, just after they had gone in, a Jew on the roof of a fort saw men in white coming out of the shimmering desert. "People of the Arabs," he shouted, "here is the one you have been waiting for!"

They rushed out, crying "Allahu akbar," and met him at the Harrah. Many of the Ansar had never seen him, and at first they greeted Abu Bakr, until the sun fell on the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr stood to shade him with his cloak. Then they knew. He was fifty-three.

He stayed in Quba' with Kulthum bin Al-Hadm from Monday to Thursday, and built the mosque of Quba', the first mosque built on taqwa after his prophethood [9:108]. 'Ali stayed three days in Makkah to give back the things people had left in the Prophet's keeping, then followed him and caught up with him in Quba'.

## madinah-entry
title: Into Madinah
word: Madinah
mood: oasis
when: Friday, 12 Rabi' al-Awwal, 1 AH
y: 622.725
place: Madinah
look: map madinah
tags: madinah, journeys
On Friday he rode on, sending for Banu An-Najjar, his mother's kin, to escort him. In the valley of Banu Salim bin 'Awf he led the Friday prayer with about a hundred men. Yathrib became Madinat Ar-Rasul, the City of the Messenger: Madinah.

Every clan he passed caught his camel's rein: "Stay with us, Messenger of Allah." "Let her go," he said each time, "she is under command." The girls of Madinah sang their welcome. The camel went on with a loose rein, knelt at the site where his mosque would stand, rose, walked a little, turned and came back to kneel in the same place. It was the quarter of Banu An-Najjar, as he had wished.

"Whose house is nearest?" he asked. "Mine, Messenger of Allah," said Abu Ayyub Al-Ansari. "Here is my house and here is my door." He stayed with Abu Ayyub. A few days later his family arrived: Sawdah, his daughters Fatimah and Umm Kulthum, Usamah bin Zaid and Umm Ayman, with Abu Bakr's household, 'Aishah among them. His daughter Zainab stayed behind with her husband Abul-'As until after Badr.
quote: Let her go: she is under command. — The Prophet ﷺ, of his camel

## fever
title: The Fever of Madinah
word: Juhfah
mood: oasis
when: 1 AH
y: 622.75
place: Madinah
look: quote
tags: madinah
Madinah was known then for its fever. Abu Bakr and Bilal both fell ill. In his fever Abu Bakr would recite that every man wakes among his family while death is nearer to him than his sandal strap. Bilal, when the fever left him, would cry out in verse for one more night in a valley of sweet grass near Makkah, one more drink at Majannah, one more sight of Makkah's hills.

'Aishah told the Prophet ﷺ, and he prayed for the city.
quote: O Allah, make it healthy, bless its measures, and take its fever away to Al-Juhfah. — The Prophet ﷺ, reported by Al-Bukhari
