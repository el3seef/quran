## marches-6ah
title: The Marches of the Sixth Year

place: Najd, the Hijaz and the north

With the Confederates broken, small columns went out one after another against the tribes still at war with Madinah. Most ended without hard fighting; they were meant to show the enemies of Islam that they could no longer stop it.
list:
- Al-Ghamr: 'Ukashah bin Mihsan against Banu Asad; they fled and left two hundred camels
- Dhul-Qassah: Muhammad bin Maslamah against Banu Tha'labah; a hundred of them fell on his men in their sleep and killed all but him, and he escaped badly wounded
- Dhul-Qassah again: Abu 'Ubaidah bin Al-Jarrah with forty men, marching all night; the enemy fled to the mountains but for one, wounded, who later became a Muslim; much was taken
- Al-Jamum: Zaid bin Harithah against Banu Sulaim, guided by a woman of Muzainah, whom the Prophet ﷺ later freed and married to one of his Companions
- Taraf: Zaid with fifteen men against Banu Tha'labah; twenty camels taken, the people fled
- Wadi Al-Qura: Zaid with twelve men to scout; they were attacked and nine were killed

## abul-as
title: Abul-'As Comes Home
word: Zainab
mood: dawn
when: Jumada al-Ula 6 AH · 627 CE
y: 627.75
place: Al-'Is, and Madinah
look: quote
tags: family, companions
Zaid bin Harithah went out with a hundred and seventy riders to Al-'Is and took a caravan of Quraish. Its leader escaped: Abul-'As, the husband of the Prophet's daughter Zainab, freed after Badr on his promise to let her go to Madinah. He took refuge with Zainab in Madinah, and begged her to ask her father for his goods back.

The Prophet ﷺ did not order it. He asked the people, and they gave back everything. Abul-'As went to Makkah, returned to every man what had been entrusted to him, accepted Islam and emigrated to Madinah. After three and a half years apart, the Prophet ﷺ gave Zainab back to him on their first marriage, for the ayah forbidding believers to marry disbelievers had not yet come down.

## khabat
title: The Army of Leaves
word: Whale
mood: oasis
when: Before Al-Hudaibiyah
y: 627.8
place: The coast of the Red Sea
look: quote
tags: battles, signs
Abu 'Ubaidah bin Al-Jarrah led three hundred riders to watch for a caravan of Quraish. Their food ran out, and they were so hungry they ate the leaves of trees, so they were called the Army of the Leaves. One man slaughtered camels for them three at a time, nine in all, until Abu 'Ubaidah stopped him.

Then the sea threw up a great whale, rich with fat, and they lived on it for half a month. When they came home and told the Prophet ﷺ, he said it was provision Allah had brought out for them, and asked if they had any of it left, so that he might eat some too.
quote: It is provision Allah brought out for you. Have you any of its meat with you, to give us some? — The Prophet ﷺ, to the Army of the Leaves

## mustaliq
title: Al-Muraisi'
word: Juwairiyah
mood: sand
when: Sha'ban 6 AH · 627 CE
y: 627.95
place: The well of Al-Muraisi'
look: quote
tags: battles, family
On 2 Sha'ban word came that Al-Harith bin Dirar, chief of Banu Al-Mustaliq, was gathering his men and other Arabs to attack Madinah. Buraidah bin Al-Husaib Al-Aslami went to see for himself, spoke with Al-Harith and heard it from his own mouth. A spy they sent towards the Muslims was caught and killed. The Prophet ﷺ left Zaid bin Harithah over Madinah and marched; the Arabs who had joined Al-Harith heard and ran for their lives.

Abu Bakr carried the standard of the Emigrants and Sa'd bin 'Ubadah that of the Helpers. They met at the well of Al-Muraisi'. Arrows flew for an hour, then the Muslims charged, and it was over. Some of the enemy were killed, their women and children taken, and much was won. One Muslim died, killed by mistake by a Helper.

Among the captives was Juwairiyah, the daughter of Al-Harith their chief, and the Prophet ﷺ married her. At once the Muslims set free a hundred of her people's captives who had become Muslims: they were now the Prophet's in-laws.
quote: They are the in-laws of the Messenger of Allah. — the Muslims, freeing the captives of Banu Al-Mustaliq

## more-honourable
title: "The More Honourable Will Drive Out the Meaner"
word: Hypocrite
mood: ember
when: Sha'ban 6 AH
y: 627.955
place: Al-Muraisi', and the road home
look: quote
tags: companions, revelation, madinah
'Abdullah bin Ubai had never forgiven the Prophet ﷺ for the crown he had lost. Each Friday he would stand before the sermon and tell the people to honour and obey this Messenger, as a mockery; after Uhud they pulled him down by his cloak, and he walked out saying he wanted no one's forgiveness. When the Prophet ﷺ married Zainab bint Jahsh, after Zaid his adopted son had divorced her, the hypocrites spread that it was a great sin, until ayahs came down to settle it [33:1].

At Al-Muraisi', 'Umar's hired man Jahjah and Sinan Al-Juhani pushed at the well. One shouted "Helpers!", the other "Emigrants!" "The calls of the Ignorance, while I am among you? Leave them: they are rotten." Ibn Ubai heard of it and burst out: they had brought this on themselves, sheltering these men and sharing their wealth with them; when they got back to Madinah, the more honourable would drive out the meaner. A boy, Zaid bin Arqam, heard him and told his uncle, who told the Prophet ﷺ. 'Umar wanted 'Abbad bin Bishr to kill him. "And have people say Muhammad kills his companions?" Instead he ordered a march at an hour no one marched, all that day and night, until they dropped asleep and forgot.

Ibn Ubai swore he had never said it, and some of the Helpers said the boy must have heard wrong. Zaid sat at home in shame, until Surat Al-Munafiqun came down with the very words: it is they who say, do not spend on those with the Messenger until they leave him, and the more honourable will drive out the meaner [63:7-8]. "Allah has confirmed what you said," the Prophet ﷺ told him. At the gate of Madinah stood Ibn Ubai's own son, 'Abdullah, a believer, with his sword drawn: his father would not enter until the Messenger of Allah gave him leave, for he was the mighty and his father the humbled.
quote: By Allah, you shall not enter until the Messenger of Allah gives you leave; for he is the mighty one, and you the humbled. — 'Abdullah, the son of 'Abdullah bin Ubai, to his father

## necklace
title: The Necklace
word: 'Aishah
mood: grief
when: On the way home from Al-Muraisi', 6 AH
y: 627.96
place: The road to Madinah, and Madinah
look: quote
tags: family, revelation, society
'Aishah was with him on this march. Near Madinah the army stopped for the night, and she went a little way off for a need. Coming back she found she had lost her necklace, a borrowed one, and went back to look for it. When she returned the army had gone; the men had lifted her litter onto the camel without noticing she was not in it, for she was young and light. She sat down alone and wept until sleep took her.

Safwan bin Al-Mu'attal, riding at the rear, knew her, for he had seen her before the veil was commanded. He set her on his camel and walked, leading it, all the way to Madinah. 'Abdullah bin Ubai and the hypocrites made a scandal of it, and some Muslims were drawn in. The Prophet ﷺ took counsel; the Aws and Khazraj almost came to blows over it, until he calmed them.

'Aishah, knowing nothing, fell ill for a month. When she heard, she went to her parents and wept for two days and a night without sleep, until she thought her heart would burst. He came to her: if she was innocent, Allah would clear her; if not, let her ask His forgiveness. Her tears stopped. She asked her parents to answer for her; they did not know what to say. So she answered herself: if she said she was innocent, and Allah knew she was, they would not believe her; if she confessed to what Allah knew she had not done, they would. She could only say what the father of Yusuf said [12:18]. Then she lay down. And at that moment the revelation came that cleared her [24:11].

Mistah bin Athathah, Hassan bin Thabit and Hamnah bint Jahsh, who had spread it, were flogged eighty lashes. 'Abdullah bin Ubai was not; but from then on his own people despised him. "Do you see, 'Umar?" the Prophet ﷺ said a month later. "Had I had him killed that day, noses would have quivered for him; now, if I ordered it, they would kill him themselves."
quote: Patience is most fitting, and it is Allah whose help is sought against what you describe. — 'Aishah, in the words of the father of Yusuf

## dream-umrah
title: A Dream of the Ka'bah
word: Ihram
mood: white
when: Monday, beginning of Dhul-Qa'dah 6 AH · March 628 CE
y: 628.2
place: From Madinah to Dhul-Hulaifah
look: quote
tags: journeys, faith, treaties
He dreamed that he and his Companions entered the Sacred Mosque in safety and did 'Umrah, their heads shaved and their hair cut. He told them, and they were overjoyed.

He washed his clothes and mounted his camel Al-Qaswa'. With Ibn Umm Maktum over Madinah, or Numailah Al-Laithi, he set out on a Monday early in Dhul-Qa'dah with fourteen or fifteen hundred men, and Umm Salamah. Many of the Bedouin stayed home. They carried no arms but a traveller's sheathed sword. At Dhul-Hulaifah the sacrificial camels were garlanded and they all put on ihram, so that everyone could see they had come as pilgrims.

A scout brought word that Quraish had gathered their allies and blocked the road. He asked his Companions; they would fight no one unless they were stopped from the House. Quraish sent Khalid bin Al-Walid with two hundred horsemen to catch them at their noon prayer; the ayahs of the prayer of fear came down, and the Muslims turned off by a rough, rocky track, and Khalid rode back to tell Quraish.
quote: We would fight no one, unless they stop us from the House. — The Companions, asked what they should do

At Thaniyat Al-Murar his camel knelt and would not get up. He swore that whatever plan Quraish offered him that honoured the sanctity of Allah's House, he would accept. Then he urged her on, and she leapt up.

## envoys
title: "Never Have I Seen a King So Loved"
word: Envoys
mood: parchment
when: Dhul-Qa'dah 6 AH
y: 628.202
place: Al-Hudaibiyah
look: quote
tags: treaties, makkah
Budail bin Warqa' of Khuza'ah came to ask what he wanted. Only to visit the House, he said. If Quraish would accept the faith, as others had, they were welcome; but if they barred the way, he would fight them to the last, and Allah's command would be done. Budail took the message to Quraish, who did not want to hear it. They sent Mikraz bin Hafs: "a treacherous man," the Prophet ﷺ said as he came, and gave him the same message.

Next came Al-Hulais bin 'Alqamah, an ally of theirs. He was so moved by the Muslims' reverence for the House that he went back and warned Quraish he would break with them if they barred people come to honour it. Then came 'Urwah bin Mas'ud Ath-Thaqafi. "Muhammad, you have gathered a rabble to set against your own kin. By Allah, I think they will desert you tomorrow." Abu Bakr answered him sharply. As 'Urwah talked he took hold of the Prophet's beard, and Al-Mughirah bin Shu'bah, standing over him in a helmet with his sword, struck his hand with the scabbard.

'Urwah watched them closely, and went back to Quraish.
quote: I have been to Chosroes, to Caesar and to the Negus in their kingdoms, and never have I seen a king among his people as Muhammad is among his Companions. When he makes wudu' they will not let its water fall to the ground; when he speaks they lower their voices. They will never give him up. He has offered you a fair plan: take it. — 'Urwah bin Mas'ud, to Quraish

## ridwan
title: The Pledge Under the Tree
word: Ridwan
mood: oasis
when: Dhul-Qa'dah 6 AH
y: 628.204
place: Under a tree at Al-Hudaibiyah
look: quote
tags: treaties, companions, revelation
He asked 'Umar to go to Quraish. 'Umar asked to be excused: Quraish hated him, and he had no strong kin in Makkah to protect him. Send 'Uthman, he said, whose family was among the most powerful there. 'Uthman went to Abu Sufyan and the chiefs: they had come only to visit the House and worship there, and would leave in peace. He was to call them to Islam, and to give the believers hidden in Makkah the good news that victory was near. Quraish would not move. But they told 'Uthman he could go round the House himself. "How could I, when the Messenger of Allah has been kept from it?"

He was gone long, and word spread that he had been killed. The Muslims gave the Prophet ﷺ their pledge that they would not run, some that they would die. Abu Sinan Al-Asadi was the first; Salamah bin Al-Akwa' pledged three times, at the beginning, the middle and the end. Then he held out his own hand: "This is for 'Uthman." He took the pledge under a tree, 'Umar holding his hand and Ma'qil bin Yasar holding back a branch from his face. Every man there pledged but one hypocrite, Jadd bin Qais. Then 'Uthman came back, and pledged too.

## treaty
title: The Treaty
word: Treaty
mood: parchment
when: Dhul-Qa'dah 6 AH
y: 628.205
place: Al-Hudaibiyah
look: list
tags: treaties, makkah
Seeing the Muslims ready to die, Quraish sent Suhail bin 'Amr to make peace. 'Ali wrote. "In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Most Kind," the Prophet ﷺ dictated. "I do not know Ar-Rahman," said Suhail; "write: In Your name, O Allah." The Muslims muttered; he agreed. "This is what Muhammad, the Messenger of Allah, has agreed with Suhail bin 'Amr." "If we believed you were the Messenger of Allah, we would not have kept you from the House or fought you. Write your name and your father's." The Muslims would not; so he rubbed the words out with his own hand, and dictated, "Muhammad, the son of 'Abdullah." Khuza'ah, the old allies of Banu Hashim, at once joined his side of the treaty.
list:
- The Muslims go home this year, and come back next year for three days, with no arms but sheathed swords in their bags
- No war for ten years; each side safe from the other
- Any tribe may join Muhammad's side or Quraish's
- Any man of Quraish who comes to Muhammad without his guardian's leave is sent back; any of Muhammad's who goes to Quraish is not

## abu-jandal

As the treaty was being written, Abu Jandal, Suhail's own son, stumbled into the camp in chains, worn out from the torture. "This is the first test of your word," said Suhail. The treaty was not yet signed when he came, said the Prophet ﷺ. "But its terms were agreed." Abu Jandal cried out, "Muslims, will I be sent back to the idolaters to be turned from my religion?"

His heart went out to him, but he would keep his word.

'Umar could not hold back his anger and grief. He spoke bitterly, and brought his sword close, so that Abu Jandal could seize it and strike his father. Abu Jandal spared his father, and was led away in his chains.
quote: Be patient, Abu Jandal, and hope for Allah's reward. Allah will make for you and the weak with you relief and a way out. We have made a peace with them and given them our word before Allah, and we will not break it. — The Prophet ﷺ

## manifest-victory
title: A Manifest Victory
word: Victory
mood: white
when: On the road home, Dhul-Qa'dah 6 AH
y: 628.215
place: Between Makkah and Madinah
look: quote
tags: revelation, treaties, companions
To many Muslims it felt like defeat: no 'Umrah this year, and a peace that seemed to bow to the idolaters. 'Umar could not bear it. "Are you not truly the Messenger of Allah?" "Yes." "Are we not in the right and they in the wrong?" "Yes." "Then why accept humiliation in our religion?" "I am the Messenger of Allah. I do not disobey Him, and He will help me." "Did you not tell us we would go to the House?" "Did I tell you it would be this year?" 'Umar went to Abu Bakr and said the same, and Abu Bakr answered as the Prophet ﷺ had.

Then Surat Al-Fath came down: We have given you a manifest victory [48:1]. He sent for 'Umar and told him. 'Umar was overjoyed, and full of regret; to atone for what he had said, he gave in charity, fasted, prayed and freed slaves.

And it was a victory. Quraish had recognised the Muslims as equals and given up their claim to stand between Arabia and its faith; the war they had led for years was over. With peace, people met and talked, and no one heard of Islam with an open mind but he entered it. Fourteen hundred went to Al-Hudaibiyah; two years later ten thousand marched on Makkah.
quote: We have given you a manifest victory. — Al-Fath 48:1

Back in Madinah, Abu Basir, a Muslim who had escaped from Quraish, came to him. Quraish sent two men to fetch him, and the Prophet ﷺ handed him over.
