## after-uhud
title: The Year of Betrayals

Uhud had cost the Muslims their name for strength, and enemies inside and outside Madinah grew bold. Two months after it, Banu Asad were gathering to raid the city. In Safar of the fourth year 'Adi and Qarah betrayed ten Companions, and Banu 'Amir seventy at the Well of Ma'unah. Banu An-Nadir plotted to kill the Prophet ﷺ, and Ghatafan made ready to attack. He met each in turn. His march to Hamra' Al-Asad had already begun to win back their standing.

- Banu 'Amir and Banu Sulaim, Safar 4 AH: the betrayal at the Well of Ma'unah

## rajee
title: Ar-Raji'
word: Raji'
mood: grief
when: Safar 4 AH · 625 CE
y: 625.55
place: Ar-Raji', on the road towards Makkah
look: quote
tags: battles, companions, signs
Men of 'Adi and Qarah came to Madinah saying some of their people had become Muslims, and asked for teachers. He sent six of his Companions, or ten, under Marthad bin Abi Marthad, or, in Al-Bukhari's report, 'Asim bin Thabit. At Ar-Raji', between Rabigh and Jeddah, a hundred archers of Banu Lihyan surrounded them. The Companions climbed a hill. Come down, they were told, and you will not be killed.

'Asim refused to come down. He fought until he and six others were killed. Three came down on the promise: Khubaib, Zaid bin Ad-Dathinah and one more. At once they were bound. The third cried treachery, resisted and was killed. Khubaib and Zaid had killed chiefs of Quraish at Badr, so they were taken to Makkah and sold.

Quraish sent men to bring back some part of 'Asim's body, for he had killed one of their nobles. They could not get near it: a great swarm of hornets covered it. 'Asim had vowed to Allah that no idolater would ever touch him, nor he them.
quote: Allah guards His believing servant after his death, as He guarded him in his life. — 'Umar, hearing of 'Asim and the hornets

## khubaib
title: Two Rak'ahs at At-Tan'im
word: Khubaib
mood: grief
when: 4 AH
y: 625.551
place: At-Tan'im, outside the Sanctuary of Makkah
look: quote
tags: companions, makkah, signs
Khubaib was kept prisoner until Quraish agreed to crucify him. He was seen in his bonds eating a bunch of grapes when there was not a single date to be had in Makkah: provision from Allah.

They led him out of the Sanctuary to At-Tan'im. He asked to pray two rak'ahs, and prayed them. Then he turned to them: had he not feared they would think he was afraid of death, he would have prayed longer. He was the first to make two rak'ahs a custom before being put to death. He asked Allah to count them one by one and destroy them to the last. He spoke verses: the parties had gathered their tribes around him, their women and children, while he was bound to a tall trunk; to Allah alone he complained; he did not care, if he died a Muslim, on which side he fell for Allah.

Abu Sufyan asked him whether he would not rather Muhammad were here in his place, to be beheaded, while he sat safe with his family. 'Uqbah bin Al-Harith, whose father Khubaib had killed, crucified him, and guards were set over his body; 'Amr bin Umaiyah Ad-Damri came by night, took it down and buried it. Zaid bin Ad-Dathinah was bought by Safwan bin Umaiyah and killed for his father.
quote: By Allah, I would not wish Muhammad to be where I am now, nor that even a thorn should hurt him, while I sat safe among my family. — Khubaib, to Abu Sufyan

## maunah
title: The Well of Ma'unah
word: Seventy
mood: grief
when: Safar 4 AH · 625 CE
y: 625.56
place: The Well of Ma'unah, in Najd
look: quote
tags: battles, companions
Abu Bara' 'Amir bin Malik, called the Spear Player, came to Madinah and would neither accept Islam nor reject it. If the Prophet ﷺ sent some of his Companions to Najd to call its people, he said, he hoped they would accept. "I fear the people of Najd will kill them." "They are under my protection." He sent seventy, or forty, under Al-Mundhir bin 'Amr. They were the best of the Companions in the Qur'an. By day they gathered firewood and sold it to feed the poor of the mosque; by night they prayed and studied the Qur'an.

They camped at the Well of Ma'unah and sent Haram bin Milhan with the Prophet's letter to 'Amir bin At-Tufail. 'Amir did not even look at it; he signed to a man, who drove a spear into Haram's back. Haram saw his own blood and cried, "Allahu akbar! By the Lord of the Ka'bah, I have won!" 'Amir called on Banu 'Amir to kill the rest; they would not break Abu Bara''s protection. So he called on 'Usaiyah, Ri'l and Dhakwan of Banu Sulaim, and they surrounded the Companions and killed them all. Only Ka'b bin Zaid was carried out wounded from among the dead; he was killed later, at the Trench.

'Amr bin Umaiyah and Al-Mundhir bin 'Uqbah, out with the camels, saw birds circling and came back. Al-Mundhir fought and was killed; 'Amr was taken, his head shaved, and freed to fulfil a vow of 'Amir's mother. On his way home 'Amr killed two men of Bani Kilab as they slept, not knowing the Prophet ﷺ had given them his protection. "You have killed two men whose blood money I must pay," he told him.

He grieved for Ma'unah and Ar-Raji' as for nothing else. For a month, in every Fajr prayer, he called on Allah against Ri'l, Dhakwan, Lihyan and 'Usaiyah, who had disobeyed Allah and His Messenger, until words came down that they recited for a time, before they were lifted from the Qur'an.
quote: Tell our people that we have met our Lord; He is pleased with us, and has made us pleased. — words revealed of the martyrs of Ma'unah, recited for a time and then lifted

## nadir
title: Banu An-Nadir

y: 625.62
place: The quarter of Banu An-Nadir, Madinah

tags: battles, treaties, people, revelation
By their treaty, Banu An-Nadir were bound to share the blood money of the two men 'Amr bin Umaiyah had killed. The Prophet ﷺ went to ask them for it with Abu Bakr, 'Umar, 'Ali and a few others. They agreed, and asked him to sit and wait beside a wall of their houses. Then they met apart. 'Amr bin Jahsh offered to climb up and drop a great stone on his head. Sallam bin Mishkam warned them that Allah would tell him, and that it would break the treaty. Jibreel did tell him. He rose at once and hurried back to Madinah with his Companions, and told them on the way.

He sent Muhammad bin Maslamah to them: leave Madinah within ten days, or be killed. 'Abdullah bin Ubai sent to say stay, he had two thousand men to help them, and Quraizah and Ghatafan would come too [59:11]. Huyai bin Akhtab believed him and answered, "We will not leave our homes. Do what you will." "Allahu akbar," said the Prophet ﷺ, and the Muslims with him.

He besieged them for six nights, or fifteen, 'Ali carrying the standard. They shot from their forts behind their palm groves, and he had palms cut down and burned [59:5]. Quraizah stayed out of it, and neither Ibn Ubai nor Ghatafan came, like Satan, who tells man to disbelieve and then disowns him [59:16]. Fear entered their hearts, and they asked to be let go. They could take all their camels could carry, but no weapons. They loaded six hundred camels, even with their doors and roof beams, wrecking their houses as they went. Surat Al-Hashr came down about them.
list:
- Where they went: Huyai bin Akhtab and Sallam bin Abul-Huqaiq to Khaibar; others to Syria
- Two who believed: Yamin bin 'Amr and Abu Sa'd bin Wahb kept their wealth
- Left behind: fifty coats of mail, fifty helmets, three hundred and forty swords, their land and houses
- Taken without fighting: so it was the Prophet's to give; he gave it to the first Emigrants and to two poor Helpers, Abu Dujanah and Sahl bin Hunaif, kept his family's provision for a year from it, and spent the rest on arms

## badr-again
title: Badr, the Appointment
word: Appointment
mood: sand
when: Sha'ban 4 AH · January 626 CE
y: 626.05
place: Badr
look: quote
tags: battles
A year had passed since Uhud, and the appointment Abu Sufyan had called out from the mountain came due. The Prophet ﷺ marched to Badr with fifteen hundred men and ten horses, 'Ali with the standard and 'Abdullah bin Rawahah over Madinah, and waited.

Abu Sufyan set out with two thousand men and fifty horses. At Majannah, near Marr Az-Zahran, a little way from Makkah, his nerve failed. He told Quraish that only a green year would do for them, a year when their animals grazed and gave milk; this was a year of drought, so he was going home, and so should they. They turned back without a word.

The Muslims stayed eight days at Badr and traded there, and came home with good profit. The balance turned back their way, and all Arabia knew it. It is called Badr the Appointment, the Second Badr, the Last Badr and the Lesser Badr.
quote: This is a year of drought. I am going back, and I bid you go back with me. — Abu Sufyan, at Majannah

## dumah

y: 626.62
place: Dumat al-Jandal, near Syria

After six quiet months, word came that the tribes around Dumat al-Jandal, on the edge of Syria, were robbing travellers and gathering to attack Madinah. It lay fifteen days' march from Madinah, and five from Damascus. He left Siba' bin 'Arfatah Al-Ghifari over the city and set out with a thousand men, with a guide of Banu 'Udhrah named Madhkur, marching by night and hiding by day so the enemy would not hear of them.

They fled before he came, leaving their herds, and the people of Dumah scattered. He stayed five days, sending out parties to find them, and found no one. On the way home he made a treaty with 'Uyainah bin Hisn. The hypocrites were quieter now, one Jewish tribe was gone and the others kept their treaties outwardly, the Bedouin were subdued, and Quraish showed no wish to fight. It was the calm before the greatest storm of all.

## confederates
title: The Confederates Gather

mood: ember

y: 627.1
place: Makkah, Najd and Khaibar
look: text

The Jews of Banu An-Nadir, now in Khaibar, watched for the Muslims to fall, and the Muslims did not fall. So twenty of their chiefs went to Makkah and offered Quraish their full support if they would attack Muhammad, and Quraish, who had not dared to come to Badr, took it. Then the same men went to Ghatafan, and to tribe after tribe, until all Arabia that hated Islam was joined against it.

They came from the south and the east at an agreed time and met outside Madinah. They were more than all the people of Madinah together, women, children and old men included. Had they come by surprise, they might have destroyed them all. But the city was watching, and word of the march came in time.

- The Muslims: three thousand

## trench
title: Salman's Trench
word: Khandaq
mood: sand
when: Shawwal 5 AH
y: 627.12
place: The north of Madinah
look: map trench
tags: battles, companions
He called a council, and after long talk they took the advice of Salman Al-Farisi. In Persia, Salman said, when they were besieged they dug a trench. Nothing like it had been seen in Arabia. Madinah was walled on the other sides by lava fields, hills and palm groves; only the north lay open, and that was the way the army would come. So the trench was dug there.

The work was shared out: forty yards to each ten men. He dug with them, carried earth with them, and kept their hearts on the Hereafter. Sahl bin Sa'd remembered them digging while others carried the earth away on their backs. They were so hungry they were near starving, and still they worked, by day, going home at night, until it was done.
facts:
- Length: about five and a half kilometres
- Width: about four and a half metres
- Depth: about three metres
- Who dug: everyone, the Prophet ﷺ with them

## rock
title: The Rock and the Meal
word: Rock
mood: dawn
when: As the trench was dug
y: 627.13
place: The trench
look: quote
tags: signs, battles
Jabir bin 'Abdullah saw how hungry the Prophet ﷺ was, and killed a lamb and had a little barley ground, and quietly invited him with a few men. He called out all the diggers, thousands of them, and they ate until they were full, while the pot still bubbled with meat and the dough was still being baked. A woman brought a handful of dates; he spread them on his cloak and called the Companions to eat, and the dates went on increasing until they spilled over its edge.

A great rock stopped the digging, and their picks would not break it. He took the pick, said "In the name of Allah," and struck. "Allahu akbar! I have been given the keys of Syria. By Allah, I can see its red palaces from here." He struck again: "Allahu akbar! I have been given Persia. By Allah, I can see the white palace of Al-Mada'in." The third blow broke the rest of it: "Allahu akbar! I have been given the keys of Yemen. By Allah, I can see the gates of San'a' from where I stand."

## siege
title: Ten Thousand at the Trench
word: Siege
mood: iron
when: Shawwal – Dhul-Qa'dah 5 AH · 627 CE
y: 627.15
place: Mount Sala', Madinah
look: quote
tags: battles, revelation
Quraish camped at Majma' Al-Asyal, Ghatafan near Uhud. The believers saw them and said, this is what Allah and His Messenger promised us [33:22]; the hypocrites said Allah and His Messenger had promised them nothing but delusion [33:12]. Three thousand Muslims took their stand with their backs to Mount Sala' and the trench before them. Quraish came up to it and stopped, amazed. They had never seen such a thing.

So they laid siege, riding up and down it looking for a weak place, while the Muslims kept them off with arrows. Then 'Amr bin 'Abd Wudd, 'Ikrimah bin Abi Jahl and Dirar bin Al-Khattab found a narrow place and spurred their horses across it, and 'Amr called for single combat. 'Ali went out, and killed him, and the others fled back over the trench.

It was so hard that prayers were missed. 'Umar came cursing Quraish: the sun had set and he had not prayed 'Asr. "By Allah, nor have I," said the Prophet ﷺ, and went down to Buthan, made wudu' and prayed 'Asr after sunset, then Maghrib, and prayed against them. The fighting was mostly arrows across the trench; six Muslims were killed and ten idolaters. An arrow cut an artery of Sa'd bin Mu'adh.
quote: O Allah, if any fighting with Quraish remains, keep me alive for it; and if You have ended it, let this wound open and let my death be in it; and do not let me die until my heart is at rest about Banu Quraizah. — Sa'd bin Mu'adh, wounded at the Trench

## betrayal

word: Betrayal

y: 627.16

Huyai bin Akhtab went to Ka'b bin Asad, chief of Banu Quraizah, who were bound by treaty to help the Muslims. Ka'b would not listen at first. Huyai talked of the chiefs of Quraish at Al-Asyal and Ghatafan at Uhud, sworn to finish Muhammad, and promised that if they went home he would come into Ka'b's fort and share his fate. Ka'b gave way, and broke the treaty. The Muslims' women and children were now open behind them.

Safiyah bint 'Abdul-Muttalib was in a fort with Hassan bin Thabit and the women and children when she saw a Jew circling it. Hassan said he was not the man for it. She took a tent pole, went down and killed the Jew with it, and Quraizah thought after that the forts were guarded. The Prophet ﷺ sent the two Sa'ds, 'Abdullah bin Rawahah and Khawwat bin Jubair to learn the truth, telling them not to spread it among the people if it was true, so as not to frighten them, and to say so openly if it was false. It was true, and Quraizah told them to their faces there was no treaty any more. The eyes grew wild and hearts rose to the throats [33:10-11], and some asked leave to go home, saying their houses lay open, when they did not [33:12-13].

He lay wrapped in his cloak a long while. Then he rose: "Allahu akbar! Good news, Muslims, of Allah's victory and help." He sent men back to guard the families, and thought of offering Ghatafan a third of Madinah's dates to go home. He asked the two Sa'ds.
It was only for their sake, he told them, when he saw all the Arabs shooting at them from a single bow.
quote: If this is Allah's command, we obey. If it is something you do for our sake, we do not need it. We knew these people in our days of idols: it is not our dates they want, but our end. Now that Allah has honoured us with Islam, we will give them nothing but the sword. — Sa'd bin Mu'adh and Sa'd bin 'Ubadah

Nu'aim bin Mas'ud of Ghatafan came to the Prophet ﷺ by night: he had become a Muslim, and his people did not know it. Let him do whatever he could, he was told.

He went to Banu Quraizah as an old friend: Quraish and Ghatafan were not like them, he said; this was not their land, and if things went badly they would go home and leave Quraizah alone with Muhammad. Let them fight only if Quraish gave them some of their nobles as hostages. Then he went to Quraish: Quraizah regretted breaking with Muhammad, he said, and had promised to hand him nobles of Quraish to be killed; give them no hostages. And he told Ghatafan the same.

On a Saturday night Quraish and Ghatafan sent to Quraizah: attack tomorrow. Quraizah answered that they did not fight on the Sabbath, and would not fight at all without hostages. Then Nu'aim was right, said Quraish and Ghatafan, and refused; then Nu'aim was right, said Quraizah. Each side now trusted the other no more. And the Prophet ﷺ prayed.

Then Allah sent a wind, with rain and bitter cold, that tore up their tents and overturned their pots. That freezing night the Prophet ﷺ sent Hudhaifah bin Al-Yaman across to bring news of them. He found them packing to leave, with nothing to show for a month.

## quraizah
title: Banu Quraizah
word: Quraizah
mood: iron
when: Dhul-Qa'dah 5 AH · 627 CE
y: 627.22
place: The forts of Banu Quraizah
look: quote
tags: battles, treaties, companions
The very day he came home, as he was washing in Umm Salamah's house, Jibreel came to him: this was no time to put away his sword. Let him go to Banu Quraizah; Jibreel would go ahead with the angels to shake their forts and cast fear into their hearts. He had it called out: no one is to pray 'Asr except at Banu Quraizah. On the way the time for 'Asr came. Some prayed on the road; others would not pray until they arrived. He blamed neither.

He set out with three thousand men on foot and thirty horses, Ibn Umm Maktum over Madinah, 'Ali ahead with the standard, close enough to hear Quraizah reviling the Prophet ﷺ. They were besieged twenty-five days. Their chief, Ka'b bin Asad, put three choices to them: accept Islam, which their own books told them was true; kill their women and children and fight to the death; or attack on the Sabbath, when they would not be expected. They would take none of them.

They asked for Abu Lubabah, their old ally, to advise them. The men pleaded with him and the women and children wept round him, and when they asked his advice, he pointed to his throat: death was waiting for them. At once he knew he had betrayed his trust. He went straight to the Prophet's mosque, tied himself to a pillar, and swore no one should untie him but the Messenger of Allah. "Had he come to me, I would have asked forgiveness for him," the Prophet ﷺ said; "now it is for Allah to turn to him." When 'Ali and Az-Zubair came forward, 'Ali swearing he would take their fort or die as Hamzah had died, they surrendered to the Prophet's judgment.
quote: If he had come to me, I would have asked Allah to forgive him; but since he has tied himself of his own will, it is for Allah to turn to him. — The Prophet ﷺ, of Abu Lubabah

The men were bound and the women and children kept apart. The Aws, their old allies, begged him to be lenient with them. Would they accept one of their own as judge? They would; and he named Sa'd bin Mu'adh, who lay wounded in Madinah. Sa'd was brought on a donkey, his people pleading with him all the way to be kind to his old allies. ## abu-rafi

Sallam bin Abul-Huqaiq, called Abu Rafi', had gathered the Confederates and paid for their army. The Aws had rid the Muslims of Ka'b bin Al-Ashraf; the Khazraj asked leave to match them, and were given it, so long as no woman or child was harmed.

Five men under 'Abdullah bin 'Atik went to his fort at Khaibar. At sunset, as the herds came in, 'Abdullah wrapped himself in his cloak by the gate as if answering a call of nature; the gatekeeper called him in, and he hid. ## thumamah
title: Thumamah
word: Thumamah
mood: dawn
when: Muharram 6 AH · 627 CE
y: 627.42
place: The Prophet's mosque, Madinah
look: quote
tags: companions, people
Muhammad bin Maslamah went with thirty men against Banu Bakr bin Kilab in Najd, scattered them and came back with spoils, and with a prisoner they had come upon: Thumamah bin Uthal, chief of Banu Hanifah of Al-Yamamah. They tied him to a pillar of the mosque.

"What have you to say, Thumamah?" the Prophet ﷺ asked him. "Good, Muhammad. If you kill, you kill a man whose blood will be avenged. If you are generous, you are generous to one who will be grateful. If you want wealth, ask, and you will have what you wish." Three days he asked, and three days Thumamah said the same. On the third he set him free. Thumamah went a little way off, washed, came back, and bore witness that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is His Messenger.

He went on to Makkah for 'Umrah. Quraish said he had left his religion. He had become a Muslim, he said, and by Allah, not one grain of wheat would reach them from Al-Yamamah until the Prophet ﷺ allowed it. He kept his word, until the Prophet ﷺ himself asked him to let the grain go.

## lihyan
title: Batn Gharran
word: Gharran
mood: grief
when: Rabi' al-Awwal or Jumada al-Ula 6 AH · 627 CE
y: 627.58
place: Batn Gharran
look: text
tags: battles
Banu Lihyan had betrayed his Companions at Ar-Raji'. While Quraish and the Arabs still sought his blood, he had not wanted to go so deep towards Makkah. Now, with the Confederates broken, he set out with two hundred men, giving out that he was going towards Syria, then turned south to Batn Gharran, where his Companions had been killed, and prayed for Allah's mercy on them.

Banu Lihyan had heard and fled into the mountains. He sent ten horsemen on to Kura' Al-Ghamim, near enough to Makkah for Quraish to hear of it, and came home after fourteen days. Then the small marches went on, one after another, into the lands of those who still made war on Madinah.
