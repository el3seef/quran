## after-badr
title: Enemies on Every Side
word: Four
mood: ember
when: Shawwal 2 AH · April 624 CE
y: 624.26
place: Madinah
look: list
tags: battles, madinah
Badr was a victory all Arabia heard of, and it made the new faith four kinds of enemy. Quraish burned for revenge. The Jews of Madinah saw every Muslim victory as a blow to themselves [5:82]. The hypocrites, led by 'Abdullah bin Ubai, raged in secret. And the Bedouin around Madinah, who lived by raiding, feared a strong state would put an end to their raids.

The first news after Badr was that Banu Sulaim were gathering men to attack. The Prophet ﷺ struck first, at their own watering place, Al-Kudr. They fled before he arrived. He stayed three days, and the Muslims came home with five hundred camels, two for each man after the fifth was set aside. It was a week after Badr.
list:
- Quraish: open calls for revenge, and every resource gathered to silence Islam
- The Jews: open hostility and resentment
- The hypocrites: Islam on their tongues, plots in their hearts
- The Bedouin: indifferent to belief, afraid for their raiding

## umair
title: A Poisoned Sword
word: Sword
mood: dusk
when: After Badr, 2 AH
y: 624.27
place: Makkah and the mosque in Madinah
look: quote
tags: companions, signs
In Makkah, 'Umair bin Wahb sat alone with Safwan bin Umaiyah, mourning the dead of Badr. 'Umair's son was a captive in Madinah. Were it not for his debts and his family, he said, he would go and kill Muhammad. Safwan took his debts and his family on himself, and asked him to keep it secret. 'Umair dipped his sword in poison and rode to Madinah.

'Umar saw him at the door of the mosque, knew he meant harm, and brought him in by his sword belt. 'Umair greeted the Prophet ﷺ in the old way of the Ignorance; Allah had given them a better greeting, he was told, the greeting of the people of Paradise: peace. Why had he come? To see that his captive son was treated well. And the sword? What good had swords done them? Then the Prophet ﷺ told him what had passed between him and Safwan when they sat alone. 'Umair stood amazed and bore witness that he was the Messenger of Allah, praising Allah for guiding him.

The Prophet ﷺ told his Companions to teach him the faith and the Qur'an, and to free his son. In Makkah, Safwan waited daily for news that would bury the shame of Badr. What came was word that 'Umair was a Muslim. 'Umair went back to Makkah and called people to Islam, and many believed through him.
quote: Teach your brother his religion and the Qur'an, and free his captive for him. — The Prophet ﷺ, of 'Umair bin Wahb

## qainuqa
title: The Market of Banu Qainuqa'
word: Qainuqa'
mood: iron
when: 15 Shawwal 2 AH · April 624 CE
y: 624.3
place: The quarter of Banu Qainuqa', Madinah
look: quote
tags: battles, treaties, people
The Muslims kept every word of the treaty with the Jews. Before Badr, an old man named Shas bin Qais, seeing the Aws and Khazraj sitting together as friends, sent a youth to sit among them and recite the poems of the War of Bu'ath, until the old hostility flared up between them. The Prophet ﷺ came with some Emigrants. Would they go back to the ways of the Ignorance, he asked them, while he was still among them, after Allah had joined their hearts? They saw it was a trick of their enemies, embraced each other and went home.

After Badr, Banu Qainuqa', goldsmiths and smiths with seven hundred fighting men, the boldest of the Jews of Arabia, were the first to break the treaty. He gathered them in their market and urged them to accept Islam before what befell Quraish befell them. They answered that he had only beaten a people who knew nothing of war; if he fought them, he would learn what men were [3:12-13]. He held back.

Then an Arab woman sat at a goldsmith's in their market, and the goldsmith secretly fastened the hem of her garment, so that when she stood she was uncovered, and they laughed. A Muslim killed the goldsmith; the Jews killed the Muslim. On 15 Shawwal he besieged their forts for fifteen days, Hamzah carrying the standard, until they surrendered to his judgment. 'Abdullah bin Ubai, their old ally, pleaded for them. He spared their lives. They gave up their wealth and weapons and were sent out of Arabia to Syria.
quote: Would you go back to the ways of the Ignorance while I am among you, after Allah has guided you to Islam and joined your hearts? — The Prophet ﷺ, to the Aws and Khazraj

Abu Sufyan swore after Badr that he would not wash until he had his revenge. He came with two hundred men, but dared not attack by day. He slipped into Madinah by night and went to the Jews: Huyai bin Akhtab would not open his door, but Sallam bin Mishkam, a chief of Banu An-Nadir, took him in, fed him and told him all he knew. Late that night his men raided Al-'Uraid on the edge of Madinah, burned some palms and killed two Muslims, then ran. ## kab
title: Ka'b bin Al-Ashraf
word: Ka'b
mood: night
when: 14 Rabi' al-Awwal 3 AH · 624 CE
y: 624.7
place: Ka'b's fort, south-east of Madinah
look: quote
tags: battles, people
Ka'b bin Al-Ashraf, a poet, rich and handsome, of Tai' by his father and Banu An-Nadir by his mother, hated Islam more than any of the Jews. When the news of Badr came he swore that if it were true, death was better than life. When it was confirmed he wrote verses against the Prophet ﷺ and in praise of Quraish, and rode to Makkah to stir them to revenge. When Abu Sufyan asked him whose religion was better guided, theirs or Muhammad's, he said theirs [4:51]. Back in Madinah he made obscene verses about the Muslim women.

"Who will deal with Ka'b bin Al-Ashraf?" the Prophet ﷺ asked. "He has spoken evil of Allah and His Messenger." Muhammad bin Maslamah offered, with 'Abbad bin Bishr, Al-Harith bin Aws, Abu 'Abs bin Jabr and Abu Na'ilah, Ka'b's foster brother, and was given leave to say what he must. He went to Ka'b complaining that this man had brought them only hardship, and asked for a loan. Ka'b asked for their women and sons as pledges; they offered their weapons instead, which gave them reason to come armed.

On the night of 14 Rabi' al-Awwal they came to his fort under the moon. His wife told him she heard the sound of murder in their voices; he said a man of honour answers a call even at night. They walked and talked, and Abu Na'ilah praised the perfume in his hair and asked to smell it, once, and then again; the second time he held his head fast, and they killed him. Al-Harith bin Aws was wounded by his own companions' swords. At Baqi' Al-Gharqad they cried "Allahu akbar," and the Prophet ﷺ, who had stayed behind praying for them, knew. He put his saliva on Al-Harith's wound, and it healed. The Jews were afraid after that, and kept to their treaties outwardly.
quote: Cheerful be your faces. — The Prophet ﷺ, as they returned; and they answered, "And yours, Messenger of Allah."

## qaradah
title: The Iraq Road
word: Iraq
mood: dusk
when: Jumada al-Akhirah 3 AH · 624 CE
y: 624.85
place: Al-Qaradah, in Najd
look: text
tags: battles, makkah
Quraish lived by their caravans, to Syria in summer and to Abyssinia in winter, and the coast road was now closed to them. With summer coming they met to find another way, and chose the long road through Najd to Iraq, with Safwan bin Umaiyah to lead and Furat bin Haiyan to guide.

Nu'aim bin Mas'ud let the plan slip while drinking, and Sulait bin An-Nu'man carried it to Madinah. The Prophet ﷺ sent Zaid bin Harithah with a hundred riders. They came upon the caravan at Al-Qaradah and took it by surprise; Safwan and his guards fled without a fight. It carried silver and goods worth a hundred thousand dirhams. Furat, captured, became a Muslim.

It was the most successful march before Uhud. Quraish's last road was closed, and Makkah's trade faced ruin. They had two choices: make peace with the new state, or crush it in one great war. They chose war.

Makkah had forbidden weeping for its dead and ransoming its captives, so the Muslims would not see its grief. Now 'Ikrimah bin Abi Jahl, Safwan bin Umaiyah, Abu Sufyan and 'Abdullah bin Abi Rabi'ah led the cry for war. They sent to Kinanah and the tribes of Tihamah for allies, and set aside the whole profit of the caravan that had escaped at Badr, a thousand camels and fifty thousand dinars, to arm the army [8:36]. At the turn of the year they marched, and took their women with them to spur the men on.

Al-'Abbas, still in Makkah, sent word of every preparation. The letter reached the Prophet ﷺ at Quba', where Ubai bin Ka'b read it to him. He asked him to keep it quiet, and hurried back to Madinah to take counsel.

## dream
title: The Dream of the Cows
word: Dream
mood: night
when: Friday, 6 Shawwal 3 AH
y: 625.2
place: Madinah
look: quote
tags: battles, companions, signs
Madinah went on alert. Men carried their weapons even at prayer. Sa'd bin Mu'adh, Usaid bin Hudair and Sa'd bin 'Ubadah kept watch through the night at his door, and armed men guarded the roads. At Al-Abwa', Hind wanted to dig up the grave of the Prophet's mother; the leaders of Quraish refused, fearing what would follow. The army came by Wadi Al-'Aqiq and camped at 'Ainain, below Mount Uhud, on Friday 6 Shawwal.

He called a council and told them his dream. Some of his Companions would be killed; one of his household would be hurt; and the armour was Madinah. His view was to stay within the city: let Quraish sit in the open, and if they came in, the men would fight them in the lanes and the women from the rooftops. 'Abdullah bin Ubai agreed, not for the plan's sake but because he did not want to fight.

But many of the best men, who had missed Badr, begged him to go out and meet them, so that Quraish would not think them afraid. Hamzah swore he would not eat until he had fought them with his sword outside Madinah. After weighing it, it was decided to go out to Uhud.
quote: I saw cows being slaughtered, and a notch in the edge of my sword, and I put my hand into a strong coat of mail. — The Prophet ﷺ, of his dream before Uhud

## armour
title: His Armour
word: Armour
mood: iron
when: Friday, 6 Shawwal 3 AH
y: 625.201
place: Madinah to Ash-Shaikhan
look: quote
tags: battles, companions
In the Friday sermon he urged them to fight and be steadfast. After 'Asr he went into his house with Abu Bakr and 'Umar, who helped him arm: his helmet, his sword, and two coats of mail, one over the other. Sa'd bin Mu'adh and Usaid bin Hudair told the people they had forced him out against his view, and when he came out they said they were sorry: if he wished to stay, they would stay.

He divided the army into three: the Emigrants under Mus'ab bin 'Umair, the Aws under Usaid bin Hudair, the Khazraj under Al-Hubab bin Al-Mundhir. A thousand men, a hundred in mail, and no horsemen. Ibn Umm Maktum stayed to lead the prayer in Madinah. On the road a well-armed troop was seen apart from the army: Jews, allies of the Khazraj. Had they accepted Islam? No. He would not seek the help of disbelievers against the idolaters, and sent them back.

At Ash-Shaikhan he reviewed the army and sent home the boys too young to fight: 'Abdullah bin 'Umar, Usamah bin Zaid, Zaid bin Thabit, Zaid bin Arqam, Abu Sa'id Al-Khudri, Al-Bara' bin 'Azib and others. Rafi' bin Khadij was allowed to stay because he was a good archer. Samurah bin Jundub protested that he could throw Rafi', so they wrestled, Samurah won, and he stayed too. Fifty men under Muhammad bin Maslamah guarded the camp that night.
quote: It is not right for a Prophet who has put on his armour to take it off until Allah has judged between him and his enemy. — The Prophet ﷺ

## turn-back
title: Three Hundred Turn Back
word: 300
mood: grief

y: 625.202

Before dawn he broke camp and prayed Fajr at Ash-Shawt, within sight of the enemy. There 'Abdullah bin Ubai turned back with three hundred men, a third of the army. They had taken his advice and not his, he said, so why should they get themselves killed? His real aim was to shake the army in front of the enemy. Two clans, Banu Harithah and Banu Salamah, nearly lost heart and went with him, but Allah held them firm [3:122].

'Abdullah bin 'Amr bin Haram, father of Jabir, followed the deserters and pleaded with them to come back and fight in Allah's way, or at least defend their homes. Had they known there would be fighting, they said, they would have come [3:167]. "May Allah drive you away, enemies of Allah," he said. "Allah will suffice His Prophet without you."

Seven hundred remained. Quraish's camp blocked the ways to Uhud, so Abu Khaithamah led them by a short path across the lava field of Banu Harithah. As they passed the garden of Mirba' bin Qaizi, a blind hypocrite, he threw dust in their faces. They rushed to kill him. "Do not kill him," the Prophet ﷺ said. "He is blind of heart as well as of eye."
quote: Come, fight in the way of Allah, or at least defend yourselves. — 'Abdullah bin 'Amr bin Haram, to those who turned back

## archers
title: The Hill of the Archers
word: Archers
mood: iron
when: Saturday morning, 7 Shawwal 3 AH · 23 March 625 CE
y: 625.203
place: The foot of Mount Uhud
look: map uhud
tags: battles
He camped with Mount Uhud at his back and his face towards Madinah, with Quraish between him and the city. Fifty archers he set under 'Abdullah bin Jubair on a low hill across the Qanat valley, about a hundred and fifty metres south-east of the army, guarding the one way round to the Muslims' rear. It was called after that day the Hill of the Archers.

His orders to them were plain: keep the horses off us with your arrows. Win or lose, do not leave this place. Even if you see the birds snatching at us, do not move until I send for you; and if you see us beat them and take the spoils, do not come down to share in them.

Al-Mundhir bin 'Amr led the right, Az-Zubair the left, with Al-Miqdad beside him, to hold off Khalid bin Al-Walid's horse. The bravest men stood in the front rank. Though he came after Quraish, he had taken the better ground: the mountain guarded his back, the archers his flank, and the enemy was left on the low ground below.

## sword

He held up a sword and asked who would take it and give it its due. 'Ali, Az-Zubair, 'Umar and others reached out for it, and he gave it to none of them. Abu Dujanah Simak bin Kharashah asked what its due was. "To strike the enemy's faces with it until it bends." "I will take it, with its due." Abu Dujanah was a proud fighter; when he tied on his red band, everyone knew he meant to fight to the death. He tied it on and strutted between the ranks. Quraish drew up in ranks: Abu Sufyan in the centre, Khalid on the right, 'Ikrimah on the left, Safwan with the foot, 'Abdullah bin Abi Rabi'ah with the archers, and the standard with Banu 'Abdud-Dar, as it had been since Qusai. Abu Sufyan goaded them: their bearer had been taken at Badr; if they could not keep it, let them hand it over. They nearly fell on him, and swore he would see what they would do.

He sent word to the Helpers to stand aside and let Quraish fight their cousins; they would not fight them. The Helpers' answer was not what he hoped. Then Abu 'Amir, once a chief of the Aws, who had left Madinah in hatred and whom the Prophet ﷺ called Al-Fasiq, the corrupt, called out to his people, sure they would come over to him. "May Allah give no comfort to your eyes, Fasiq," they answered. Behind Quraish's lines Hind and the women beat their drums and sang that they would welcome the men who fought and abandon those who fled.

## lion
title: The Lion of Allah
word: Hamzah
mood: grief
when: 7 Shawwal 3 AH
y: 625.206
place: Uhud
look: quote
tags: battles, family
The faith of the Muslims swept over Quraish like a flood; "Martyrdom!" was their cry that day. Abu Dujanah cut through their ranks with the Prophet's sword. Az-Zubair, stung not to have been given it, followed him to watch, and saw him kill every man in his way. Once he raised it over someone urging Quraish on, who shrieked: it was Hind bint 'Utbah. He lowered it. He honoured the Prophet's sword too much, he said, to strike a woman with it.

Hamzah fought like a man possessed, the strongest men scattering from him like leaves. He was not killed face to face. Wahshi, an Abyssinian slave of Jubair bin Mut'im, had been promised his freedom if he killed Muhammad's uncle, to avenge Jubair's uncle killed at Badr. Wahshi tells it himself: he hid behind trees and rocks, watching Hamzah cut men down like a dappled camel, no one able to stand before him. When Hamzah struck down Siba' bin 'Abdul-'Uzza, Wahshi balanced his spear and threw it. It went into his belly and came out between his legs. Hamzah tried to come at him and fell. Wahshi went back to the camp and sat there; he had come for no one else. In Makkah he was freed.

Years later, after Ta'if, Wahshi became a Muslim, and with the same spear he killed Musailimah the liar.
quote: I killed him only to free myself. — Wahshi bin Harb, of Hamzah

In the confusion the Muslims struck at each other. Hudhaifah saw his father Al-Yaman about to be killed by Muslims and cried out that he was his father, but they did not hear, and killed him. "May Allah forgive you," said Hudhaifah, and he gave away the blood money in charity.

Slowly they came back to themselves, and fought on, and the rumour was found to be false.

## wounded

For a moment only Talhah bin 'Ubaidullah and Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas were left with him, and Quraish closed in to kill him. 'Utbah bin Abi Waqqas threw a stone that broke his lower right tooth and cut his lip. 'Abdullah bin Shihab Az-Zuhri wounded his forehead. Sa'd shot arrow after arrow at them. "Shoot, Sa'd, may my father and mother be your ransom," he said, words he never said to any other. Talhah fought like eleven men; he caught a blow aimed at him with his hand, and his fingers were cut through. He had thirty-five or thirty-nine wounds that day, and his hand stayed lame. "Whoever would see a martyr walking on the earth, let him look at Talhah," the Prophet ﷺ said later, and whenever Uhud was mentioned, Abu Bakr said, "That day was all Talhah's." Sa'd saw two men in white fighting for him as he had never seen men fight, before or after; it is said they were Jibreel and Mika'il.

## uhud-lessons
title: Defeat, or Victory?

Was Uhud a defeat? In its second half Quraish had the upper hand and dealt the Muslims heavy losses. But they never took the Muslims' camp, took no captives and no spoils, did not pursue the army up the mountain, and left the field first; Madinah lay open a few miles away, and they dared not march on it. The next day the beaten army chased the victors, and the victors ran. Both sides had suffered, and both had hope; but the believers hoped from Allah what the others did not [4:104].

Some sixty ayahs of Surat Al 'Imran came down about it, from the morning he set out to place the believers at their posts [3:121] to its end: Allah would not leave the believers as they were until He had told the bad from the good [3:179].
list:
- The cost of disobedience: the archers' leaving their post turned a victory into loss
- The Messengers are tried: so that those who follow them for victory alone are told apart from those who believe
- The hypocrites unmasked: their withdrawal and their talk showed the Muslims the enemy within
- Victory delayed: it humbles the soul; the believers are patient where the hypocrites are not
- Martyrdom: a rank Allah grants to those He loves, and a means by which His enemies earn their end
