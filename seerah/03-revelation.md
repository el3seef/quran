As he neared forty, he took to long hours alone, thinking over the world around him. He would take barley porridge and water and go out to the hills around Makkah. His favourite retreat was a small cave on Mount An-Nur, two miles from Makkah: four cubits long and less than two wide.

He went there often, above all in Ramadan, to worship and reflect, and he fed the poor who came to him. The idols and wrongs of his people weighed on his heart, but no clear way to put them right had yet been shown to him.

These years of solitude, three of them before the Message, were a preparation for the trust he was about to carry.

In Ramadan of his third year of solitude, the revelation came. The book fixes the night as Monday, the twenty-first of Ramadan, the tenth of August 610 CE. He was forty lunar years, six months and twelve days old.

'Aishah tells it, in Al-Bukhari. The angel came to him in the cave and said, "Read!" He said, "I cannot read." He went home trembling and said to Khadijah, "Cover me, cover me." When his fear had passed, he told her what had happened. She comforted him and took him to her cousin Waraqah bin Nawfal, an old blind man who had become a Christian and copied the scriptures.

Waraqah said this was the angel Allah had sent to Moses, and wished he could be young when his people drove him out. "Will they drive me out?" the Prophet ﷺ asked. Waraqah said that no one had ever brought what he brought without being treated as an enemy. Waraqah died a few days later.

## first-believers
title: The First Believers
word: First
mood: dawn

Makkah was the heart of idol worship in Arabia, and its people were the keepers of the idols. So the call began quietly, so as not to provoke them before it had taken root. He began at home, with those closest to him, and with those he trusted to see the truth.

Khadijah was the first to believe. Then came Zaid bin Harithah, whom Khadijah had given him and who chose to stay with him rather than go home with his own father. Then his young cousin 'Ali, who lived in his house, and his close friend Abu Bakr, all on the first day. Abu Bakr, wealthy, gentle and much visited, brought others: 'Uthman bin 'Affan, Az-Zubair bin Al-'Awwam, 'Abdur-Rahman bin 'Awf, Sa'd bin Abi Waqqas and Talhah bin 'Ubaidullah.

Others followed: Bilal, Abu 'Ubaidah bin Al-Jarrah, Abu Salamah, Al-Arqam, the three sons of Maz'un, Sa'id bin Zaid and his wife Fatimah (sister of 'Umar), Khabbab bin Al-Aratt, 'Abdullah bin Mas'ud and more. Ibn Hisham counts more than forty. They met him and learned their religion in secret.
people:
- Khadijah: his wife, the first to believe
- Zaid bin Harithah: freed by him; later fell commanding the army at Mu'tah
- 'Ali bin Abi Talib: his cousin, raised in his house
- Abu Bakr: his closest friend, whose call brought many others
- 'Uthman bin 'Affan: of Banu Umaiyah, through Abu Bakr
- Bilal bin Rabah: the Abyssinian, later the caller to prayer

The five daily prayers were not among the first commands. Ibn Hajar wrote that he certainly prayed before the Night Journey, and it is reported that prayer was then required twice a day, before sunrise and before sunset. It is reported that at the first revelation Jibreel taught him how to make wudu'.

When prayer was due, he and his Companions slipped away to the mountain passes to pray out of sight. Once Abu Talib came upon him praying with 'Ali. When he learned what they were doing, he told them to keep at it.

The surahs of those years were short, with strong rhythms and striking pauses. They called the soul to purity, warned against being taken in by the world, and described Paradise and the Fire as if they were in view.

## sorcerer
title: Sorcerer, Poet, Madman
word: Sihr
mood: ember

look: text

A few months into the open call, the pilgrimage season drew near. Quraish feared what the Arab pilgrims would hear, so they met at the house of Al-Walid bin Al-Mughirah to agree on one thing to say about him. A soothsayer? His words were not the soothsayers' rhymes. Possessed? He showed none of its signs. A poet? What he recited was nothing like poetry. A sorcerer? He used none of their spells, and his speech was sweet through and through.

At last they settled on this: his words were a magic that split son from father, brother from brother and husband from wife. The Qur'an tells how Al-Walid thought, plotted and frowned, and then called it nothing but magic [74:18-25].

At the fairs of 'Ukaz, Majannah and Dhul-Majaz, he went from camp to camp calling people to Allah, and Abu Lahab followed behind him shouting, "Don't listen to him. He is a liar."

They mocked him as a madman [15:6] and a lying sorcerer [38:4]. They laughed at the believers [83:29-33]. They called the Qur'an tales of the ancients [25:5], a lie others had helped him make [25:4], or the teaching of some man [16:103]. They asked why a messenger should eat food and walk in the markets [25:7]. An-Nadr bin Al-Harith told tales of the kings of Persia to the same crowds, and bought singing girls to draw people away [31:6].

## persecution
title: The Persecution
word: Ahad
mood: ember
when: From the 4th year of prophethood
y: 613.8
place: Makkah

tags: companions
When mockery could not stop the flow of people into Islam, the chiefs agreed on torture. Each clan was to deal with its own believers. If a man of standing came to Islam, Abu Jahl would shame him and threaten to ruin his trade. If he was weak, Abu Jahl beat him.

'Uthman's uncle rolled him in a mat of palm leaves and lit a fire under him. Mus'ab bin 'Umair's mother, who had kept him in luxury, starved him and threw him out. Bilal's master, Umaiyah bin Khalaf, had him dragged through the streets by boys with a rope around his neck. He laid him in the noon heat with a great rock on his chest, until Bilal would deny Muhammad or die. Bilal only said, "One, One." Abu Bakr bought him and set him free, as he freed others.

The family of Yasir were laid on the burning sand. Passing them, the Prophet ﷺ said, "Patience, family of Yasir: your meeting place is Paradise." Yasir died under the torture. His wife Sumaiyah was killed by Abu Jahl, the first martyr in Islam. Their son 'Ammar was tortured until he said what they wanted, though his heart never changed. Allah revealed that one forced to it, his heart at rest in faith, was excused [16:106].

- Abu Fukaihah: laid on the scorching sand under a stone; freed by Abu Bakr

## najm

Quraish had a habit of drowning out the Qur'an with noise whenever it was recited [41:26]. But in Ramadan of the fifth year, he recited Surat An-Najm in the Sacred Mosque before a crowd of their chiefs. They listened in silence, held by it. At its final words, "So prostrate to Allah and worship Him" [53:62], they all fell down in prostration, believers and idolaters alike.

## negus
title: Before the Negus
word: Negus
mood: white
when: In Abyssinia
y: 615.2
place: The court of the Negus, Abyssinia
look: quote
tags: journeys, letters
Quraish sent 'Amr bin Al-'As and 'Abdullah bin Abi Rabi'ah, both still idolaters, with rich gifts for the king and his bishops. They asked him to hand over the refugees, who had left their fathers' religion and did not follow his either.

The Negus called the Muslims. Ja'far bin Abi Talib spoke for them. They had been an ignorant people, he said: they worshipped idols, ate dead meat, did shameful things, cut their ties of kinship, wronged their neighbours, and the strong fed on the weak. Then Allah sent them a messenger whose lineage, truthfulness, trustworthiness and purity they knew. He called them to worship Allah alone. He told them to be truthful, keep trusts, honour kinship, be good to their neighbours and spare blood. He forbade lewdness, false witness, consuming the orphan's wealth and slandering chaste women. For believing him, their people had tortured them, so they had come to his land, choosing him over all others.

Asked to recite something of what had come from Allah, Ja'far recited the beginning of Surat Maryam [19:16-26]. The Negus and his bishops wept until their beards were wet. This and what Jesus brought, he said, came from the same source of light. He would not hand them over.

The next day the envoys said the Muslims spoke ill of Jesus. Ja'far said they held what their Prophet ﷺ taught: that Jesus was Allah's servant and messenger, His spirit and His word cast into the Virgin Mary [4:171]. The Negus said Jesus was no more than that. He gave back the gifts and sent the envoys away, and the Muslims lived in peace in his land for years.

One day Abu Jahl came upon the Prophet ﷺ at As-Safa and abused him and his religion. He said nothing in reply. Abu Jahl struck his head with a stone and drew blood, then went to sit with Quraish at the Ka'bah.

His uncle Hamzah was coming home from hunting, bow over his shoulder, when a servant girl who had seen it all told him. ## umar
title: 'Umar Becomes Muslim
word: 'Umar
mood: dawn
when: Dhul-Hijjah, 6th year of prophethood
y: 616.12
place: Makkah
look: quote
tags: companions
Three days after Hamzah, 'Umar bin Al-Khattab became Muslim. He was the most feared man in Makkah and a fierce enemy of the new faith. The Prophet ﷺ had prayed that Allah would strengthen Islam with whichever of two men He loved more: 'Umar, or Abu Jahl.

One day 'Umar came upon him praying at the Ka'bah, reciting Surat Al-Haqqah. "A poet," he thought. "It is not the word of a poet," came the next verse. "A soothsayer," he thought. "Nor the word of a soothsayer" [69:40-43]. Islam entered his heart, he later said, but his pride held it back.

One day he set out, sword in hand, to kill the Prophet ﷺ. A man stopped him on the way: his own sister Fatimah and her husband Sa'id had become Muslims. 'Umar burst into their house, where Khabbab was teaching them Surat Ta-Ha. He struck Sa'id, then struck his sister and drew blood. They told him to do as he pleased, for they believed. Seeing his sister's blood, 'Umar softened and asked for the page. She told him to wash first. He read until "Truly I am Allah, there is no god but Me, so worship Me and establish prayer for My remembrance" [20:14]. "How beautiful this is," he said. "Take me to Muhammad."

At the house by As-Safa, a Companion saw 'Umar with his sword through the door. Hamzah said, "Let him in. If he comes for good, good. If not, we will kill him with his own sword." The Prophet ﷺ took him by his garment and asked why he had come. "To believe in Allah and His Messenger." The Companions cried "Allahu akbar" so loud it was heard at the Ka'bah. Soon the Muslims went out in two rows, Hamzah leading one and 'Umar the other, and prayed openly at the Ka'bah. From then on he was called Al-Faruq, the one who sets truth apart from falsehood.
quote: We were never able to pray at the Ka'bah until 'Umar became Muslim. — 'Abdullah bin Mas'ud

## utbah

After Hamzah and 'Umar, Quraish saw that torture would not work and tried temptation. 'Utbah bin Rabi'ah, one of their chiefs, sat with him and made an offer. If he wanted wealth, they would make him the richest of them. If he wanted honour, they would make him their chief. If he wanted to be king, they would crown him. "Have you finished, Abul-Walid?" he asked. Then he recited the opening of Surat Fussilat [41:1-5]. 'Utbah listened, leaning back on his hands. 'Utbah went back to his friends with a changed face. He had never heard such words, he said: not poetry, not magic, not soothsaying. They should leave him be. If the Arabs finished him, they were spared him. If he prevailed, his glory would be theirs. They said he had been bewitched.

One evening the chiefs gathered behind the Ka'bah and sent for him. They made 'Utbah's offers again. He told them he wanted none of it. He was a messenger delivering his Lord's message. Then they demanded signs. Let his Lord move the mountains away, level their land, and bring their dead back to life. Let Him send an angel to vouch for him, and give him gardens, treasures and palaces of gold. Or let a piece of the sky fall on them. "That is for Allah," he said. "If He wills, He does it." He left them grieved.

Abu Jahl swore he would crush the Prophet's head with a rock while he prostrated. The next morning he went toward him carrying the rock, but turned back pale and shaking and let it fall. He said a monstrous camel with terrible teeth had come at him. The Prophet ﷺ said it was Jibreel, and that if Abu Jahl had come closer, he would have seized him.

## boycott

Quraish met at Khaif Bani Kinanah, in the valley of Al-Muhassab, and swore a pact against Banu Hashim and Banu Al-Muttalib. No one would buy from them or sell to them, marry them, mix with them, visit them or speak to them, until they handed Muhammad over to be killed. They wrote it on a sheet and hung it inside the Ka'bah. Ibn Al-Qayyim says the man who wrote it was Baghid bin 'Amir, and that his hand was later paralysed after the Prophet ﷺ prayed against him.

Abu Talib withdrew with both clans, Muslim and not, into the valley of Abu Talib on the edge of Makkah. They stayed there from Muharram of the seventh year until the tenth: three years. Food was cut off. Quraish bought up whatever came into Makkah so none could reach them. They ate leaves and animal skins, and the cries of hungry children could be heard outside. Only now and then did a kind relative smuggle food in. Abu Jahl once caught Hakim bin Hizam bringing wheat to his aunt Khadijah, and only another man's intervention let it through.

At night Abu Talib had his nephew sleep in his own bed. When the rest were asleep, he moved him to another, in case of an assassin. The Prophet ﷺ still went to the Ka'bah to pray, and in the sacred months he still called the pilgrims to Allah.

How did the early Muslims hold firm through such torment? The book names the roots of it.

Their enemies feared him even as they fought him. Abu Jahl admitted they did not call him a liar, only what he brought [6:33].
