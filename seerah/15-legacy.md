In Makkah his household was he and Khadijah bint Khuwailid. He married her at twenty-five, when she was forty; she was his first wife, and while she lived his only one. She bore him sons and daughters. None of the sons lived long.

His daughters were Zainab, married before the Hijrah to her maternal cousin Abul-'As bin Ar-Rabi'; Ruqaiyah and then, after her death, Umm Kulthum, married to 'Uthman bin 'Affan; and Fatimah, married to 'Ali bin Abi Talib between Badr and Uhud, the mother of Al-Hasan, Al-Husain, Zainab and Umm Kulthum. His son Ibrahim, born to Mariyah the Copt, died an infant in Madinah.

He was permitted more than four wives, for various reasons. In all he married thirteen women. Eleven he lived with; two of these, Khadijah and Zainab bint Khuzaimah, died before him, and nine survived him. Two marriages, one to a woman of Banu Kilab and one to Al-Jauniyah of Kindah, were never completed.

## why
title: Why So Many
word: Ties
mood: dusk
when: After the Hijrah
y: 633.01
place: Madinah
look: quote
tags: family, society
He spent his young manhood, nearly thirty years of it, married to one wife older than himself, Khadijah, and then to Sawdah. His later marriages were not the marriages of desire. By marrying 'Aishah and Hafsah he bound himself to Abu Bakr and 'Umar, as by giving Fatimah to 'Ali and Ruqaiyah and Umm Kulthum to 'Uthman he bound himself to them: the four whose sacrifice for Islam is known to all.

Among the Arabs, kinship by marriage was a bond no one would shame by fighting it. Umm Salamah was of Makhzum, the clan of Abu Jahl and Khalid bin Al-Walid; after he married Umm Habibah, her father Abu Sufyan never again met him with hostility; when he married Juwairiyah, the Companions freed a hundred of her people. And he had a whole people to teach, women as well as men, and his wives, trained by him, carried what they learned of his life to everyone; 'Aishah above all.

His marriage to Zainab bint Jahsh was commanded to end a deep-rooted custom of the Ignorance: an adopted son counted as a true son, in marriage, divorce and inheritance. He hesitated, fearing what the hypocrites would say, and was rebuked for it [33:37]. So the custom was broken by his own act: call them by their fathers' names [33:5]; Muhammad is not the father of any of your men, but the Messenger of Allah and the Seal of the Prophets [33:40].
quote: When Zaid had ended his marriage to her, We married her to you, so that the believers should have no difficulty concerning the wives of their adopted sons. — Al-Ahzab 33:37

## dates-and-water

His home was hard to live in. As far as Anas knew, he never tasted a thin flattened loaf in his life, nor saw a roasted goat with his own eyes. "Two months would pass," said 'Aishah, "and we would see three new moons, and no fire was lit in the houses of the Messenger of Allah." What did they live on? "The two black things: dates and water."

Of all his wives, only once did any complain in a way that called for a ruling. The ayahs gave them their choice: if they wanted this world and its glitter, he would provide for them and release them kindly; if they wanted Allah, His Messenger and the Home of the Hereafter, a great reward awaited them [33:28-29]. Not one chose the world. And when small jealousies arose among them, as among any wives, and Allah disapproved [66:1-5], they stopped.
quote: The two black things: dates and water. — 'Aishah, asked what the Prophet's household lived on

No one was ever so loved. Those who knew him would have given their lives to save a fingernail of his from harm. Those who saw him described him so.
list:
- 'Ali: neither very tall nor very short; his hair neither tightly curled nor straight; his face somewhat round; his eyes black and large, with long lashes; when he walked he lifted his feet as if going down a slope; when he turned, he turned his whole body. Whoever saw him suddenly stood in awe of him; whoever came to know him loved him
- Al-Bara': of medium height, broad-shouldered, his hair to his earlobes; I never saw anyone more handsome. Was his face bright as a sword? "No; like the moon"
- Jabir bin Samurah: I looked at him on a moonlit night in a red garment, and at the moon, and to me he was more beautiful than the moon
- Abu Hurairah: I never saw anything more beautiful; it was as if the sun moved across his face; and no one walked faster: we wore ourselves out, and he was at ease
- Ka'b bin Malik: when he was happy his face shone like a piece of the moon
- Anas: I never touched silk softer than his palm, nor smelled musk or amber sweeter than his scent; at his death there were about twenty grey hairs in his head and beard
- Jabir: any road he had taken, those who came after knew by its fragrance that he had passed that way
- The seal of prophethood: between his shoulders, towards the left, the size of a pigeon's egg

## exalted
title: An Exalted Standard of Character
word: Character
mood: white
when: All his life
y: 633.04
place: Makkah and Madinah
look: quote
tags: character
He spoke the purest Arabic, and every tribe's dialect with its own people. Patience and forgiveness came to him most when he had the power to punish; the more he was harmed, the gentler he grew. Given a choice between two things, said 'Aishah, he always chose the easier, unless it was a sin, and then he was furthest of all from it; he never took revenge for himself, only when Allah's sanctity was violated, and then for Allah. He was the last to anger and the first to be pleased.

He gave like a man who did not fear poverty; in Ramadan, when Jibreel came, he was more generous than the free wind, and no one ever asked him for anything and heard "no". When the fighting was fiercest, 'Ali said, they sheltered behind him, and he was always nearest the enemy. One night Madinah woke to a noise and the men ran out towards it, and met him coming back, alone, on Abu Talhah's unsaddled horse, his sword round his neck: there was nothing to fear.

He was shyer than a girl kept in her chamber. Even before prophethood they called him Al-Amin, and Abu Jahl himself said, "We do not call you a liar; we only deny what you bring" [6:33]. He forbade people to stand for him as men stood for kings; he visited the sick and the poor, accepted a slave's invitation, mended his own sandals and his own clothes, and milked his own sheep. On a journey, when one man offered to slaughter the sheep, another to skin it and another to cook it, he said he would gather the wood; they could do it for him, he knew, but he did not want to be set above them.
quote: And you are surely on an exalted standard of character. — Al-Qalam 68:4

## gatherings
title: His Gatherings
word: Gentle
mood: oasis
when: All his life
y: 633.05
place: Madinah

tags: character, society
Hind bin Abi Halah described him: always thoughtful, never at rest; silent for long, speaking only when there was need, and then clearly, with full meaning and no excess. He made much of every blessing, however small; if he did not like a food, he neither praised it nor found fault with it. He never grew angry for himself; only for Allah's sanctity and His religion. His laughter was mostly a smile.

He sat wherever there was room at the edge of the gathering, and told others to do the same. He gave each person there his share of his attention, so that each thought no one was more honoured by him. Whoever came to ask his advice spoke first and spoke last, and he listened patiently until the man had finished; and he never sent anyone away without what he asked for, or a gentle word. His gathering was one of kindness, modesty, patience and honesty: no voices raised, nothing sacred made light of; the old respected, the young treated with mercy, the stranger cared for.

He kept himself from three things: hypocrisy, excess, and what did not concern him; and he spared people three: he did not run them down, did not shame them, and did not seek out their faults. He spoke only where he hoped for reward. When he spoke, those around him bowed their heads as if birds sat on them, and only spoke when he was silent.
quote: O Allah, send blessings upon Muhammad and the family of Muhammad, as You sent blessings upon Ibrahim and the family of Ibrahim; You are Worthy of all praise, Most Glorious. — The prayer upon him, with which the book ends
