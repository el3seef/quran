## breach
title: The Treaty Broken
word: Al-Watir
mood: night
when: Sha'ban 8 AH · 629 CE
y: 629.92
place: Al-Watir, near Makkah
look: text
tags: treaties, makkah, people
Under the treaty of Al-Hudaibiyah every tribe could join either side, and Banu Bakr joined Quraish while Khuza'ah joined the Prophet ﷺ. The old blood between them had never cooled. In Sha'ban of the eighth year Banu Bakr fell on Khuza'ah by night at the water of Al-Watir, and Quraish helped them in the dark with men and weapons. Khuza'ah fled into the Sanctuary, and even there Nawfal, chief of Banu Bakr, followed and killed them, where blood was never shed.

It was a plain breach of the treaty. Quraish knew at once what they had done, and met in alarm, and sent Abu Sufyan himself to Madinah to renew it.

Three days before the news came, he had told 'Aishah to make ready for a march. Abu Bakr found her at it and asked; she did not know where. On the tenth of Ramadan he marched with ten thousand men, leaving Abu Ruhm Al-Ghifari over Madinah. At Al-Juhfah his uncle Al-'Abbas met him with his family, coming to join him. At Al-Abwa' they came on his cousins Abu Sufyan bin Al-Harith and 'Abdullah bin Abi Umaiyah, who had mocked and harmed the believers for years, and he would not receive them. 'Ali told Abu Sufyan to come to him as Yusuf's brothers came: by Allah, Allah has preferred you above us, and we were sinners [12:91]. He did, and the Prophet ﷺ answered with Yusuf's words: no blame on you today [12:92]. Abu Sufyan bin Al-Harith became a Muslim, and a good one.

They had set out fasting. At Al-Kadid, between 'Usfan and Qudaid, he broke his fast and the army with him. They camped in the valley of Fatimah at Marr Az-Zahran, and that night he had every man light a fire: ten thousand fires, with 'Umar over the watch.

Abu Sufyan came out from Makkah to look, with Hakim bin Hizam and Budail bin Warqa'. Before they reached the camp they met Al-'Abbas, who told him how things stood: let him accept Islam and bring his people to surrender, or he would be killed. In the morning he left Marr Az-Zahran, and told Al-'Abbas to hold Abu Sufyan at a place where he could see the whole army go by. Tribe after tribe passed under its banners, and he asked of each who they were, until at last came the Prophet ﷺ in his battalion of Emigrants and Helpers, clad in iron. "Who are these?" "Muhammad and his Companions." No one could stand against them, said Abu Sufyan: "By Allah, your brother's son's kingdom has grown mighty." "It is prophethood," said Al-'Abbas. The people scattered into their houses and into the Sanctuary, but a band under 'Ikrimah bin Abi Jahl, Safwan bin Umaiyah and Suhail bin 'Amr gathered at Al-Khandamah, meaning to fight.

He sent the army in from every side. ## idols

He did not rest, but went straight to the Ka'bah with the Emigrants and Helpers around him. Three hundred and sixty idols stood round the House. He struck them one by one with his bow, reciting that truth has come and falsehood has vanished, for falsehood is bound to vanish [17:81], and that falsehood can neither begin anything nor bring it back [34:49]. Then he went round the House on his camel; he was not in ihram that day.

He called for 'Uthman bin Talhah, keeper of its key, and took the key from him. Inside were pictures, among them Ibrahim and Isma'il casting divining arrows, and he condemned what Quraish had done and had every idol broken and every picture erased. He went in with Usamah and Bilal, prayed facing the wall opposite the door, and went round inside saying "Allahu akbar" and declaring Allah's oneness.

## free

Then he stood on the step of the Ka'bah's door and looked down at Quraish crowding the Sanctuary below. There is no god but Allah alone, he said: He has kept His promise, helped His servant and alone defeated the parties. Every claim of privilege, of blood or of wealth, was beneath his feet, but the keeping of the House and the giving of water to pilgrims. The blood money for a man killed by a whip or a stick, even half by intent, was heavy: a hundred camels, forty of them in calf.

Allah had taken from them the pride of the Ignorance and its boasting of fathers: all people were from Adam, and Adam from dust. And he recited: We created you from a male and a female and made you nations and tribes that you might know one another; the noblest of you with Allah is the most mindful of Him [49:13].

"People of Quraish, what do you think I will do with you?" "Good: you are a noble brother, the son of a noble brother." He gave the key back to 'Uthman bin Talhah, and the water of the pilgrims to Al-'Abbas.

## second-day

word: Sanctuary
mood: white

On the second day he stood and praised Allah, and declared Makkah sacred until the Day of Judgment. No blood was to be shed in it. If anyone claimed leave to fight there because the Messenger had fought, let him know that leave had been given to the Messenger alone, for an hour of one day. Its thorn trees were not to be cut nor its game disturbed, nor its lost things picked up except by one who would announce them. Then he went up As-Safa and, facing the House, raised his hands in prayer. The Helpers round him whispered that now Allah had given him his own city, he would stay. He asked what they were saying, and they told him.

Makkah saw that its way now lay through Islam, and its people came to give their pledge: first the men, then the women, 'Umar beside him to take their words. ## hawazin
title: Hawazin Gather
word: Awtas
mood: ember
when: Shawwal 8 AH · 630 CE
y: 630.08
place: Awtas, in the land of Hawazin
look: quote
tags: battles, people
The conquest of Makkah stunned Arabia, but the proud tribes of Hawazin and Thaqif would not bow. Nasr, Jusham, Sa'd bin Bakr and Banu Hilal rallied to Malik bin 'Awf An-Nasri, and he made them bring their wives, children and herds with them to Awtas, a valley in their land near Hunain, so that they would fight to the death for them.

Old Duraid bin As-Simmah, famous for his wisdom in war, asked which valley they were in. Awtas: good ground for horses, he said, neither rocky nor soft; but why did he hear camels groaning, donkeys braying, children crying and sheep bleating? Malik explained. "You are nothing but a shepherd," said Duraid. Can anything stop a beaten man from running? If you win, all that helps you is a man with his sword and spear; if you lose, you shame your people and lose your wealth. Send the families back to safety. Malik would not: let Hawazin obey him, or he would fall on his sword. Hawazin said they would obey.

His spies came back from the Muslim camp with their limbs trembling. They had seen men of distinction on piebald horses, they said, and could not help what had come over them. The Prophet ﷺ sent Abu Hadrad Al-Aslami to live among Hawazin and bring back word.
quote: This is a day I have not missed, and yet I will not be sorry to miss it. — Duraid bin As-Simmah, at Awtas

Nineteen days after he entered Makkah he marched out with twelve thousand men: the ten thousand of the conquest, and two thousand more, most of them Makkans newly Muslim. He borrowed a hundred coats of mail from Safwan bin Umaiyah, and made 'Attab bin Asid governor of Makkah. That evening a rider came: he had climbed such and such a mountain and seen all Hawazin gathered with their camels, cattle and sheep. The Prophet ﷺ smiled: they would all be the Muslims' spoils tomorrow, if Allah willed. Anas bin Abi Marthad stood guard that night.

## ambush
title: Ambush in the Valley

mood: night
when: Before dawn, Shawwal 8 AH
y: 630.1

Malik bin 'Awf had gone into the valley by night and hidden his men in its ravines and narrow places, with orders to rain stones and arrows on the Muslims the moment they came in sight, and then charge together. Before dawn the Prophet ﷺ set out his ranks and banners, and in the half-dark the army began to go down into the valley, knowing nothing. As they were making camp the arrows came, and then Hawazin, and the Muslims turned and ran in confusion.

The rout was so complete that Abu Sufyan bin Harb, newly Muslim, said they would not stop running until they reached the sea, and Kaladah bin Al-Junaid said the magic had failed today. He urged his mule towards the enemy, calling out in verse, and his cousin Abu Sufyan bin Al-Harith held its rein and Al-'Abbas its stirrup, trying to hold it back. Then he dismounted and asked his Lord for help: "O Allah, send down Your help."

## taif-siege
title: The Siege of Ta'if
word: Ta'if
mood: dusk
when: Shawwal 8 AH
y: 630.12
place: Ta'if
look: map hunain
tags: battles, signs
Most of Hawazin and Thaqif shut themselves up in Ta'if with Malik bin 'Awf. Khalid went ahead with a thousand men; the Prophet ﷺ followed through Nakhlah Al-Yamaniyah, Qarn Al-Manazil and Liyyah, where he had Malik's fort pulled down, and camped by Ta'if and laid siege to it, for between ten and twenty days.

Arrows came down from the walls like locusts. Many were wounded and twelve killed, and the camp moved back to the higher ground where the mosque of Ta'if stands today. He set up a catapult and battered a breach; some men crept to the wall under a wooden shelter to burn it, and the defenders poured red-hot iron on them, and shot them as they ran. He ordered their vines cut and burned; they begged him by Allah and their kinship to stop, and he stopped. His caller cried that whoever came down from the fort would be free, and twenty-three came. One let himself down from the wall on a pulley-wheel, so he called him Abu Bakrah, the man of the wheel; he gave each of them to a Muslim to look after.

The fort held, with a year's food inside. He asked Nawfal bin Mu'awiyah Ad-Daili, who said: a fox in its hole; wait and you will catch it, leave it and it will do you no harm. He told 'Umar to announce they would leave in the morning. The men protested: leave without taking it? "Then fight in the morning." They fought and were wounded. When he said again that they would leave tomorrow, they were glad, and he laughed. As they rode away he told them to say: returning, repenting, worshipping, and praising our Lord. Asked to curse Thaqif, he prayed for them instead.
quote: O Allah, guide Thaqif, and bring them to us. — The Prophet ﷺ, asked to curse Thaqif

He waited ten nights at Al-Ji'ranah before dividing anything, hoping Hawazin would come repentant and take back what they had lost. No one came. Then he gave first, and most, to those new to Islam, to win their hearts: to Abu Sufyan a hundred camels and forty ounces of gold, and when he asked for his son Yazid, the same, and for Mu'awiyah, the same again; to Hakim bin Hizam a hundred, and when he asked, a hundred more; to Safwan bin Umaiyah a hundred, and another, and another; to Al-Harith bin Kildah a hundred; to other chiefs of Quraish and the tribes, a hundred, fifty, forty.

The Helpers had stood with him at Hunain when others ran, and now they watched those who had run go away with full hands, while they had nothing. Some of them grumbled that he had found his own people again. Sa'd bin 'Ubadah told him. "And you, Sa'd?" "I am one of my people." "Gather them for me."

He came to them and praised Allah. Had he not come to them astray, and Allah guided them; poor, and Allah enriched them; enemies, and Allah joined their hearts? "Allah and His Messenger are more generous and more gracious." Why did they not answer him? He answered for them: they could have said, and said truly, that he came to them rejected and they believed him, deserted and they helped him, a fugitive and they sheltered him, destitute and they comforted him. Did they resent a little of this world, with which he had won people to Islam, when he had left them to their Islam?

"Are you not content, Helpers, that the people go home with sheep and camels, while you go home with the Messenger of Allah? By Him in whose hand is my soul, but for the Hijrah I would be one of the Helpers. If the people took one valley and the Helpers another, I would take the valley of the Helpers. O Allah, have mercy on the Helpers, and their sons, and their sons' sons." They wept until their beards were wet. "We are content with the Messenger of Allah as our portion."

When it was all divided, fourteen men of Hawazin came as Muslims, led by Zuhair bin Surad, his foster uncle among them, and spoke so movingly that he was touched. "You see who is with me. The best speech to me is the truest. Which is dearer to you: your wealth, or your women and children?" Nothing, they said, was equal to kin. After the noon prayer, he told them, stand and ask the Messenger to intercede with the believers, and the believers with the Messenger, for your captives.

They did. Whatever was his and Banu 'Abdul-Muttalib's, he said, was theirs. "And ours is the Messenger's," said the Emigrants and the Helpers. Al-Aqra' bin Habis refused for Banu Tamim, 'Uyainah bin Hisn for Fazarah, and Al-'Abbas bin Mirdas for Banu Sulaim, whose men said, "No, ours is the Messenger's," and he cried that they had undermined him. He told them that anyone who would not give up a captive freely would have six for each from the next spoils, and everyone gave theirs up, 'Uyainah last of all with an old woman he had held on to. He gave each captive a garment.

Then he put on ihram at Al-Ji'ranah and went to Makkah for 'Umrah, left 'Attab bin Asid as its governor, and came home to Madinah in the last six nights of Dhul-Qa'dah, in the eighth year.
